# Choose a region with one rendering of the sketch and verify it with another

On the complete benchmarks, pooling zoomed regions helped. Crops chosen retrospectively with labels had considerably more headroom. A hypothesis is that the best-matching part of a sketch is inflated when the same noisy CLIP measurement both selects the location and gives the photo its score.

Read each of thirteen regions with frozen CLIP and repeat after reflecting that same region. These are two correlated views of one sketch. In a candidate's target word distribution, let $L_{hri}$ be the likelihood of region $r$ at pose $h$ for photo $i$. Normalize the selector by its mean over the unlabelled gallery to suppress regions activating many different candidates. Use a prior with equal probability for the two window sizes and equal probability for the locations of each size. For candidate $i$, use the first pose to choose a soft distribution of regions and the other pose to read those regions, then exchange their roles. Ordinary pooled evidence, the same selection and readout in a single pose, and a wrong correspondence within each size are matched controls.

As a second readout, weigh the held-out regional evidence by whether the *same* candidate also receives unusually strong support there relative to the other locations. In the two-pose design the average likelihood ratio against every reordering of the second pose's windows within a size is exactly one when each stratum uses its own mean. This tests correspondence and isolates it from the number of views. Also test reversing the held-out association and replacing the candidate-dependent selector with the globally most confident CLIP response.

Use the previous historical word + RGB gallery term as a global score. Independently tune the ordinary pooling and the same-pose alternative, including whether the global reading uses both poses. Evaluating on both full Sketchy splits precedes TU with locked settings. Attribute to the proposed mechanism only the change above these equally expensive controls. Cross-view agreement alone cannot prove independent visual observations or identify a human-drawn object part.

The regional selection/classification separation is related to WSDDN. Its trained streams are replaced here with candidate-conditioned held-out checks inside a single frozen RGB retriever. FreeDom and the RGB gallery operator supply the other two direct precedents.
