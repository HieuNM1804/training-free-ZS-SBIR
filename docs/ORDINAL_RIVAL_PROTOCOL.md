# Ask a sketch which of two descriptions better separates its plausible gallery rivals

On the original word–diffusion rankings, grouping scored photos by their true class **only for retrospective diagnosis** shows useful information beyond the first choice. The correct class is among the three largest class-mean scores for 91.6%, 90.5%, and 94.1% of queries on Sketchy Ext-1, Ext-2 and TU respectively. It is the largest for only 81.8%, 73.2% and 78.8%. The algorithm never receives these true groupings or their names. We hypothesize that transferring a few reproducible binary preferences from actual gallery rivals is more reliable than comparing the absolute image–text scores of different domains.

Use candidate image groups discovered by spherical k-means on RGB or RGB plus semantic features. Between each pair A and B, select candidate words on their respective sides using the positive term contributions to Jensen–Shannon divergence between their mean lexical profiles. For a pair of opposing terms u and v, estimate the frequency of the ordinal event "u scores above v" independently on two gallery halves within each group. Use Jeffreys smoothing for Bernoulli counts. A test receives weight only if the difference between A and B has the same sign on both halves. Weight it by the smaller absolute difference. A maximum-weight bipartite assignment selects disjoint term pairs. This makes it impossible for one highly confident term to occupy every selected comparison. These disjoint comparisons are not assumed to represent statistically independent measurements.

A single sketch answers each comparison using its own source-prompt logits. Compute its empirical log-odds for A versus B from the group-specific probabilities of that binary answer. Strictly increasing recalibrations of the complete query word-score vector leave the ordinal comparisons invariant. Candidate-specific rivals are weighted by their probability under the initial retrieval. Find class log scores whose differences agree with the transported pair decisions in product-weighted least squares. For skew-symmetric pair margins $b_{AB}$ and a normalized current rival profile $\rho$, a centered optimum has $h_A=\sum_B\rho_B b_{AB}$. This solves all pairwise inconsistencies without an iterative optimizer. Compare adding the ordinal evidence and replacing the original between-group evidence while preserving the within-group ordering. No training, no gallery labels and no other queries enter the scorer.

Search group count and geometry, 4 versus 18 disjoint tests, native or gallery-centered source offsets, rival weights and fusion on the two Sketchy splits. Compare a balanced pilot, then its five leading configurations on all Sketchy examples, and lock before TU. Full ablations include uniform rivals, continuous instead of ordinal answers, weights without reproducibility magnitude and unrelated source word identities. Class-based accuracy, cluster purity and top-3 class rank are diagnostics only and cannot be used as retrieval algorithms.

Relative attribute orderings are known in zero-shot vision. Transporting within-image rank-order decisions across measurement platforms is known in statistical classification, and combining pair decisions into a coherent multiclass estimate is established. The research hypothesis is the construction of diagnostic, disjoint, independently reproduced tests **for the particular competing unlabeled target hypotheses of one image query** and their closed-form rival-weighted projection. The historical baseline contributes its public word bridge and RGB diffusion.
