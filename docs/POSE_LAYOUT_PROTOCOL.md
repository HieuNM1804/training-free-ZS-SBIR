# Matching layout evidence that reproduces across physical views of one query

A first-order layout contrast helped a promising Sketchy pilot. A softmax comparison with four arbitrary permutations can still reward an unusually poor negative or one lucky orientation. A full-picture rendering can change word evidence through interpolation, boundaries, orientation and texture. This new hypothesis requires the *same arrangement of parts* to beat alternative arrangements under different physical poses of the **same individual** sketch.

Form a cyclic group $G$ of four permutations of the 49 native patch locations. Its generator permutes 48 positions in twelve fixed four-cycles and fixes one location. This preserves every native patch exactly. Also form the physical rotations/reflections $H$ of the square. Encode $h(gx)$, so a counterfactual indexed by $g$ describes the same original arrangement at every pose. When the image is rotated first, use the conjugate $hgh^{-1}$ to reassign its now physically rotated patch contents. This distinction matters for testing whether an apparent reproduction is about the same layout.

For word $w$ and pose $h$, let $r_{hg}=|G|\exp(z_{hg})/\sum_{g'}\exp(z_{hg'})$. If the originally designated layout were uniform in the finite orbit, a single normalized response would have mean one. Two views of the very same sketch are dependent. Multiplying their nominal evidence as if independent would generally be miscalibrated. Instead integrate products **only over distinct physical poses** and **the same counterfactual layout index** to obtain $T_g=\sum_{h\ne h'} r_{hg}r_{h'g}$. Use $E=|G|T_{\mathrm{id}}/\sum_g T_g$ as a word-specific reproduction score. Its mean over all four designations of the original layout is exactly one for every image and word, without probabilistic independence among poses. It is bounded by four. This is a model-relative finite-orbit statement and does not claim that natural sketches have uniform layouts.

Rank the full gallery using the original public word likelihood modulated by this evidence. Compare within the same pose set and gallery index with augmentation alone, mean single-pose evidence, squaring the same pose, and pooling the contrast without distinguishing the matched pose/layout identity. Also compare with the simpler original-layout log-ratio from the previous branch. Geometry, source vocabulary and gallery data remain frozen. A ranking improvement already reproduced by pose aggregation or by one-pose contrast weakens the new likelihood's interpretation. Select on complete Sketchy splits after a balanced pilot; keep TU-Berlin for locked transfer.

The scientific question is whether geometry-dependent evidence can be certified against a **finite model counterfactual** and still improve retrieval for abstracts of photographs. The word bridge and gallery operator have established precedents. Finite-group evidence and its normalization also have a statistics literature. The specific paired pose–layout design and the use of a bounded, dependence-corrected contrast at each retrieval word are the adaptation.
