# Does changing the depiction of a public name distort the order of real matches?

A sketch of one object often scores highly against several public names. Replacing the word “sketch” with “photo” in the prompt can shift the scores unequally across those names. The experiment asks whether that name-dependent change causes missed relevant photos. If it does, a small change to the visual readout that suppresses the change across prompts should repair retrieval while preserving the sketch's content. The two templates for each domain, the 20,931-name Open Images dictionary, frozen OpenAI CLIP ViT-B/32 and the historical RGB gallery operator are fixed.

For each sketch, decompose its native visual embedding into the direct updates of 48 heads in its last four attention blocks. Propose names by the average of the two prompted similarities. For each proposed name take the midpoint of its two text vectors and their signed difference. Center the responses over the proposed names. A constant preference for one depiction carries no information about their relative ranking.

Let H contain the observed head directions. The local edit is q′ = q + Hᵀb. Minimize the squared change in centered midpoint response plus a ridge penalty and λ times the remaining centered response to the domain differences. This gives one small linear system per sketch. Estimate the edit on alternate ranks in the name panel, and shrink it according to the same objective on the other half. Exchange halves and average. Restore the original spread of midpoint scores. This is an intervention on the direct visual readout after the original forward pass. Downstream in-network effects of changing a head during the forward pass are outside the experiment.

Only the global lexical input of the frozen regional retriever changes. For each gallery candidate, add 0.65 times the new minus old log likelihood under the same gallery word index. The retained regional term and the graph stay fixed. Each query uses its own image, the public prompt banks and the unlabelled photo gallery.

The controls independently optimize (i) ordinary interpolation toward the midpoint prompt, (ii) a common text-derived subspace with the same within-image head Gram matrix, (iii) a rotation of the visual directions in embedding space preserving their geometry, (iv) replacement of the target noun inside each panel with preserved contrast lengths, (v) skips of cross-fold shrinkage, and (vi) the opposite edit. We also compare all controls at the chosen primary's settings. Rotating the ambient directions changes their relationship to text. Merely relabeling heads would leave the solution invariant.

Use 36 sketch queries per class at the previous fixed sample seed and every gallery photo. The 128 plausible names at ranks 193–320 never enter either half of any fit. Measure actual change in their domain variance, midpoint correlation and the association of the change with per-query AP. A lower held-out domain variance can coexist with worse retrieval. Report the count of corrected and newly broken top-one matches and the post-hoc rank of the literal category word where it has one unambiguous public spelling. Category names enter this last measurement only after scores are fixed. The strongest evidence for this mechanism would combine reduced held-out variance, better retrieval than the tuned simple controls and a compatible full-data result. Select using the two Sketchy splits, then test TU-Berlin only if the study warrants expansion. Both datasets share the direction sketch → photo.
