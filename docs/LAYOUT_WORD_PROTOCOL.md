# A shared word can be supported by layout or local appearance

A public word can be evidence for a sketch because of the arrangement of its parts, while receiving its gallery support mostly from local patches, texture or context. Identity-based pairing and whole-embedding gallery diffusion cannot tell which visual evidence produced the matching word. The new study estimates a word-specific response to the same intervention in both domains, using only frozen CLIP ViT-B/32.

For each image, retain every one of its 49 native 32-by-32 patch embeddings. Reassign the contents to four fixed random permutations of their positions before adding CLIP's original positional embeddings. Encode each arrangement with the original visual transformer. As a numerical control, jointly permuting the *complete tokens* and their positions keeps the CLS output unchanged up to floating point. This separates the intervention from changes in input content or from forbidden edge extraction. Unusual layouts are a model probe. They do not represent a sample of natural sketches.

At word $w$, form the log likelihood of the observed layout relative to the average word likelihood under the four arrangements. Let $a_x(w)$ be the logistic transform of this log-ratio with equal prior odds. It is a bounded sensitivity score for CLIP's response to this intervention. In the gallery compute $\pi_w=\sum_i p_i(w)a_i(w)/\sum_i p_i(w)$. For a shared word, the proposed two-channel readout multiplies its original contribution by
$$
1+\lambda\,\frac{(a_q(w)-\pi_w)(a_i(w)-\pi_w)}{\pi_w(1-\pi_w)}.
$$
At unit strength this is the likelihood ratio for a shared binary route versus independent binary routes when both posteriors are calibrated to the same prior. Calibration across domains is a modeling assumption. For the actual gallery, the word's $p_i(w)$-weighted average multiplier is exactly one for any query. Query rankings can change, but increasing the global mass of a popular word cannot explain the new scores. Two full dot products on precomputed gallery tables are enough to score all candidates. A word can be affected even when it is the only one supported by the sketch. This differs from the prior minimum-collision coupling.

Compare matched routes, opposite routes, gallery-independent layout contrast on the sketch alone and on the photos alone, simple layout contrast on both, the fixed historical named word/RGB diffusion baseline, rerandomizing the target sensitivity across photos while preserving its weighted word prior, and independent permutation subsets. Primary selection uses only the two Sketchy splits, then full 30-class TU transfer. Compare gallery-level score contributions separately from new information supplied by the intervention. All raw images and all encoders stay within the agreed protocol.

Shuffling patch layout has prior uses. Sain et al. train for SBIR with patch shuffling in their fine-grained setting. Visual Contrastive Decoding contrasts an original and a distorted input for language generation. The test here is the per-word, two-image match of counterfactual routes under a gallery-mass conservation identity, evaluated in zero-shot retrieval without training. The original public vocabulary and gallery operator come from [FreeDom, WACV 2025](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/WACV2025/html/Efthymiadis_Composed_Image_Retrieval_for_Training-Free_Domain_Conversion_WACV_2025_paper.html) and [Wang et al., CVPR 2012](https://pages.ucsd.edu/~ztu/publication/cvpr12_CrDP.pdf).
