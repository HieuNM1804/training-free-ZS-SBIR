# Can two images support the same public word for the same internal reason?

A complete word score can conceal conflicting visual evidence. In previous full-split diagnostics, 383 Sketchy Ext-2 queries whose strongest regional word match was wrong still returned the correct category at rank one under native visual cosine. Adding a fixed amount of cosine or a gallery-calibrated same-photo verifier corrected some of these queries, with little overall benefit. The hypothesis here concerns the computation of each public word score inside CLIP. Distinct images can have equal image–text similarity while relying on different heads and residual updates.

Decompose one native OpenAI ViT-B/32 forward into the individual CLS attention contributions of the final four visual blocks, their four MLP updates, and the earlier CLS stream. Preserve the actual post-LayerNorm centering, image-specific normalization and learned projection. Keep the earlier stream, biases and measured floating-point remainder in one complementary component. Verify the sum against the frozen unmodified embedding, including after storing individual slots. This is an additive decomposition of an observed forward pass. It describes direct contributions and does not measure the causal effect of ablating a head.

For each public name and each component, estimate on unlabelled target photos the part of its word contribution that would be expected from the complete word score alone. Subtract this conditional linear prediction separately for the source and target prompt banks. A gallery residual for a word has zero empirical correlation with the full word score. If every head contributes only a fixed affine function of that word's total score, the remaining evidence is zero. Use the source sketch to nominate names from the complete approved dictionary. For each proposed word, check if the photo's residual component pattern agrees with the sketch's. Average the agreement over the sketch's soft word posterior and add it to the independently locked regional word score.

The gallery computation is moment estimation, without model gradients or labeled pairs. A factorized query representation can compute the word-conditioned component agreement with all photos using matrix multiplication, including for names outside the compact gallery index. Compare the same head measurements before removing the total word score, complete word scores without heads, a within-block permutation of head identities, and coarsening to block-level contributions. A useful independent effect must exceed the strongest simpler measurement. Test on the two exploratory Sketchy splits and transfer only after freezing the configuration. The labels enter evaluation after the ranking.

The prior decomposition was introduced to interpret CLIP and is not claimed as new. The proposed mechanism checks if two inputs from different domains agree on the decomposition of a public-name score *conditional on that score*, under target-gallery statistics. This operational claim can fail if residual head agreement reflects style, or succeeds at same-photo matching rather than category retrieval. The public lexical and gallery diffusion system provides the starting rank list.
