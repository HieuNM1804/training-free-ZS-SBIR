# Does a photo already supply some of the evidence in its printed name?

A one-view sketch needs at least 42 and 7 ranked public nouns to reach Semantic Dot +4 under a perfect sense selector on the two full Sketchy splits. With a matched source photo the corresponding bounds are 5 and 2. This motivates moving the naming step entirely to the gallery. The bounds give the selector class labels and do not predict this pilot's mAP. [Nomination evidence](results/description-choice-blocks/README.md)

For each photo take the canonical public noun of its first WordNet definition using the one-view photo and the exact frozen drawing prompt from the preceding study. Define V(I,w) by placing the complete image in a 192×192 square centered above a 32-pixel printed word strip in a white 224×224 image. Text is black on white in one fixed Pillow default font. For the null image or word, leave its compartment white. Immediately before the frozen visual tower's final LayerNorm, read the final CLS, subtract its channel mean, multiply by the released LayerNorm gain and apply the native 768×512 visual projection. This is a linear readout of the final CLS. Subtraction therefore precedes output normalization. Use all the usual internal CLIP blocks.

The gallery vector is G(I) = [V(blank,w) − V(blank,blank)] − [V(I,w) − V(I,blank)]. Its sign is fixed before scoring. If the photo already evokes the printed concept, part of the effect of introducing the word on a blank may disappear when the photo is present. Under any additive model V(I,w) = A(I) + B(w), the vector is exactly zero. Non-additivity alone does not imply object grounding. At query time obtain the ordinary native image embedding of the single original sketch and score its cosine to each gallery vector. There is one vector per photo and one ordinary CLIP pass per sketch. Target naming plus two content-bearing canvases take three image passes per gallery photo for the method. Blank word cards are shared. No labels, other sketches or photo graph enter this indexing rule.

Run one configuration on both full unseen Sketchy splits, seed 42, using the original KD-SBIR evaluator. Report the released Semantic Dot reference, native RGB cosine, the text-free photo canvas, the joint photo-and-word canvas, the effect of the printed word alone, the corresponding photo-prompt text embedding alone, and the identical interaction with names permuted among photos within the split. The permutation preserves the frequency of every name. Keep any unchanged assignment and report its frequency. Use the pilot's noun bank and its frozen source prompting even if the photo name is visibly wrong. Any numerical zero interaction has score zero against every query.

To ask whether G separates different photographs that received exactly the same word, preselect one uniformly indexed positive by a seed-42 hash of the original query ID among same-class photos excluding its exact source. Within the same printed noun, seek a wrong-class photo immediately above and one immediately below that positive by native visual cosine. Require both, report retained counts, and give each side half weight for every retained query. The native comparator and both name-only comparators must each have 50% accuracy. Report both sides, class/subject-balanced accuracy and the paired class-bootstrap interval (10,000 draws, seed 42). Gallery names, the fixed wrong-name permutation and all retrieval outputs precede this label-based evaluation. Test the numerical cancellation identity, norm distribution and cosine of G to its word-only control. Compare full-gallery mAP/P and paired AP changes against native, text-only, shuffled and Semantic Dot. Full-gallery success requires a positive comparison to Semantic Dot on both splits with at least about four points targeted. A flat or negative effect will be reported with the same conditional controls and closes this configuration without sign flipping, fusion, or tuning. TU is reserved for a method that passes the exploratory tests.

Synthetic class-name panels already appear in LoGoPrompt, which learns prompts for recognition. Visual and typographic signals in CLIP have also been experimentally separated. The proposed contribution would require evidence for **recovering cross-depiction visual evidence from the attenuation of a specific intervention**. Printing a name and observing a change is already established.
