# Recovering gallery evidence omitted by a compact public vocabulary

On full Sketchy, a single public word nominated by a sketch can sometimes retrieve the right photo population even when the regional word system fails. Choosing the word with hindsight among the sketch's top 16 yields 78.59 and 83.48 mAP on the two splits, versus 56.27 and 55.83 for always picking its first word. Among these hindsight choices, 67.9% and 61.6% fall outside the 64 words in the regional index. These are oracle diagnostics, not deployable scores. A word outside the compact vocabulary can be a synonym that adds no retrieval information. The new hypothesis concerns photo populations that the compact vocabulary cannot explain.

Partition unlabelled gallery photos into visual groups and split each group into two disjoint panels. In each panel j, every public word w has a probability vector pⱼ(w) over groups, obtained from the sum of the photo-to-word posteriors. Use the same panel and estimator to form the columns Aⱼ associated with the 64 incumbent public words. Ask for the largest amount of w's gallery population that can be supplied by one common nonnegative mixture of the incumbent names in both panels. Solve the linear program maximizing the sum of its coefficients a, subject to Aⱼa ≤ pⱼ(w) for every group and both panels. The zero vector is always feasible. Since every column and every right-hand side has unit mass, the represented mass lies between zero and one. Record primal feasibility, duality gaps, and tests computed by including the exact incumbent columns. The residual is u(w) = averageⱼ[pⱼ(w) − Aⱼa]. Identical or exactly convex-representable columns leave zero missing mass up to solver precision. Indexing uses no sketch or benchmark category name.

Given one sketch, the frozen source prompt bank proposes words and a probability for each. Average their residuals under these probabilities to obtain a nonnegative measure U of total mass m. If B is the group distribution implied by the locked regional retriever, replace it with (1−ηm) B + η U. Retain the regional system's ordering within each visual group by multiplying its photo probabilities by the ratio of new to old group mass. This changes group preference exactly once and recovers the starting ranking when the proposals bring no new mass.

Compare this against adding the complete word populations with the same query-specific m, adding the same populations with a freely selected constant weight, removing at most one known name per word, replacing missing mass by already-explained mass, and permuting which word contributes each residual while preserving its size. Measure whether a larger estimated missing mass predicts queries helped by the correction. Check exact alias and mass identities. A positive result requires an advantage above simpler raw expansion on both Sketchy splits and evidence on the transfer benchmark. Two folds estimate stability of the empirical explanation. They cannot prove semantic truth or the existence of an irreducible physical component in the source distribution.

The maximum removable background mass is an established question in mixture proportion estimation. The proposed use compares simultaneous explanations of a named visual population across unlabelled panels, then returns the unexplained probability to an existing cross-domain retriever. The frozen base contributes public lexical transfer and the existing gallery operator.
