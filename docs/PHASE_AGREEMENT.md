# Phase and appearance neighborhood agreement

Four octave-spaced wavelets at six orientations estimate Kovesi's contrast-normalized phase congruency. The maximum principal moment provides a spatial strength map. The input pixels, the crop derived from that map, and the two polarities of its rendering are processed by the same frozen CLIP ViT-B/32 on both sides of retrieval.

The hypothesis is that a gallery item's two representations can link neighborhoods even when a query agrees with the other representation more strongly. A query votes through four routes formed from the appearance and phase representations on the two sides. The consistency of the query neighborhoods with the gallery graph determines voting weights. There is a pure color-graph control with the same region crops, a direct-route control, and a shuffled correspondence control. The trial uses no public concept names or domain prompts.

Three studies supply mechanisms. Kovesi describes phase congruency and its orientation moments. IsoCLIP supplies the frozen projector transform. Query Specific Fusion supplies neighborhood agreement and graph-based retrieval. The native-projector variant measures the contribution of IsoCLIP. The Sketchy sweep selects by mean precision first and mean mAP second. Its held-out configuration is evaluated on the full 30-class TU gallery. Learning representations with phase-image proxies for cross-domain retrieval has prior work. The research question here concerns training-free agreement among per-query retrieval routes, with wavelet phase congruency as the common representation.
