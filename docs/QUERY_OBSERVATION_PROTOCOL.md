# A sketch as an observation constraint on each candidate photograph

In the full post-retrieval Sketchy diagnosis, 56.6–57.1% of the variance of per-query AP occurred between drawings of the same verified source photograph. Among misses with another drawing available, 90.8–91.5% had at least one sibling drawing that retrieved the category at rank one. A random sibling of a miss was correct less often, 51.5–60.4%. The query mechanism below receives one sketch only. The repeated source-object IDs belong exclusively to a completed diagnostic. [Within-object evaluation](https://github.com/HieuNM1804/training-free-ZS-SBIR/tree/research/regional-evidence-interventions/docs/results/regional-evidence-interventions)

Treat the centered native convolutional patch tokens of this sketch as the empirical feature variation available to express the object. Given a gallery photo, find the closest arrangement of real-valued patch tokens with that sketch's first two moments. This approximates what the candidate might look like under the observation constraint of this particular sketch. The construction operates before positional embeddings and keeps the CLIP transformer frozen.

If $X,Y$ are the centered 49 by 768 token matrices of sketch and photo, $H$ is an orthonormal basis of the mean-zero subspace of patch positions, and $U\Sigma V^\top$ is the SVD of $H^\top YX^\top H$, use $Z=\boldsymbol1\mu_X+HUV^\top H^\top X$. Centered $Z$ has exactly the covariance of the sketch. It minimizes squared distortion from $Y+\boldsymbol1\mu_Y$ among all 49-token matrices with these moments. Reordering the sketch's patch positions leaves this optimum unchanged when the minimizing readout is unique. The content-dependent spatial arrangement of $Z$ comes from the photo. This is a claim about moments and the optimizer. A transformed token sequence is not a ground-truth sketch.

Read the rendered candidates with the native CLIP transformer. Compare the query to the candidate before and after the moment transfer. The primary test subtracts the same change of affinity after jointly shuffling the slots of the native and rendered candidate. This difference-in-differences isolates the additional dependence on candidate layout under the sketch's moment constraint. Candidate moments are unchanged by each shuffle. Also measure the transferred affinity alone and its increment without the layout correction. Compare exact covariance transfer with channel-wise mean and variance transfer, a shared target-independent style reference, native-only evidence, and a score from patch covariances without re-encoding. The same six candidate gallery clusters and their representatives are used throughout.

The concepts of minimum-distortion style transfer and matching feature moments are established. The retrieval hypothesis is using one independently presented sketch to constrain every shortlisted target, and quantifying whether that target's geometry becomes more legible under the same query-specific observation. The approximation to a physical drawing is testable. Record full-moment and self-reproduction errors, the effect of reordering the source patches, matched controls and full Sketchy selection before a TU transfer. The first pilot uses complete Sketchy galleries and a balanced sample of 36 sketches per class.
