# Do the same visual words change together from the area around a part to the part itself?

Zooming a sketch improved the frozen word-and-gallery retriever on both complete Sketchy splits. A uniformly pooled set of zooms was competitive with interventions on the query alone. This study tests whether the missing signal is a relation between two scales in *both* the sketch and the photo. The evidence is an empirical distribution of public words over corresponding large and small windows. A patch of background, an isolated part and a complete object can give similar words when looked at at only one scale.

Use four outer 5 by 5 windows and nine inner 3 by 3 windows on the native 7 by 7 grid. There are 16 containment edges. Each edge has probability 1/16. Write this matrix as $E$ and the product of its actual row and column marginals as $E_0$. In particular, the central inner window receives more mass than a corner in both cases. A picture's outer and inner windows give matrices of CLIP word probabilities $O,I$. Its paired word distribution is $J_1=O^\top E I$ and its independent distribution is $J_0=O^\top E_0 I$. The two have identical outer and inner word marginals by construction. No coordinate in the sketch is aligned to a coordinate in a photo.

The outer words are supported by the whole-image gallery prior. Select the inner words by their within-photograph variance over the inner windows across the public vocabulary. The same word identities are used with the paired photo and sketch prompt banks. A gallery RGB operator averages the distributions across visually similar photos. For query $q$ and candidate $i$, compute four positive overlaps $S_{ab}=\langle J_a(q),T J_b(i)\rangle$, optionally weighted by the inverse product of gallery word marginals. The new score contribution is

$$\ell=\log S_{11}-\log S_{10}-\log S_{01}+\log S_{00}.$$

This ratio is one before taking logs when *either* side loses all its dependence between outer and inner words. The terms at a single scale and the one-sided correlations have explicit comparators. Gallery smoothing is applied to each distribution separately. The four terms stay well-defined under the same gallery operator. The score uses regional query retrieval as its starting point, and may include the independent two-scale overlap as a controlled marginal readout.

Compare with optimizing the independently pooled photo and sketch windows, the direct paired overlap, using dependence on only one side, a reversed edge assignment in the sketch, and using the same incorrect assignment on both sides. The wrong assignment keeps both spatial marginals fixed. Verify the word-marginal identities, and cancellation when either side is replaced with the independent distribution. The development sample is the same 36 unseen sketches per class on both Sketchy splits, with full galleries. Compare the strongest configurations on both full splits before locking for TU-Berlin. A result shared by independent pooling belongs to using zoomed gallery images. A positive contrast against that comparator and the wrong correspondences supports the spatial dependence term.

The direct precedents are public vocabulary inversion, RGB gallery diffusion and bilinear pooling of localized visual signals. The new part is a four-overlap likelihood ratio after preserving the observed word marginals and applying gallery propagation. It attributes the correction to dependence in both images. The construction assumes fixed paired domain prompts and an unlabelled photo gallery.
