# Which gallery hypotheses predict the change in what a sketch reveals at higher resolution?

Native previews improved prediction of the words a high-resolution sketch crop would produce on both development splits. Their choice of a *single* crop did not consistently beat a within-group pairing placebo. This experiment tests whether the change in evidence itself can distinguish candidates. All thirteen zooms are read. The independently locked per-candidate regional retriever supplies the base.

For one crop of one sketch let a and b be its categorical public-word readings before and after the zoom. For each unlabelled visual group g, let U_g be the gallery distribution of zoom words at that location. Obtain C_g by reweighting photos within the same group and location according to how much their native preview resembles this sketch's native preview. Read each photograph's own zoom. Two disjoint gallery panels are processed separately. Add one pseudo-observation drawn from U_g, with the effective number of neighbours as the observed sample size. This controls log instability when a few images dominate.

Score the contribution of each group by the interaction (b − a) · (log C_g − log U_g). Average this interaction over panels and the existing scale-balanced region prior. It is exactly zero if the sketch reveals no change, or if the gallery's forecast is unchanged by the preview. The difference between two groups' scores measures which candidate better predicts the direction of the newly revealed words relative to its own coarse expectation. It is a difference of log scores under a soft word observation. It is not a proof of mutual information between physical image parts.

The decisive comparison breaks the within-photo preview–zoom pairing while keeping the regional word marginals in each group and panel. Other controls read the wrong sketch preview at the same scale, drop the subtraction of the coarse sketch reading, retain only the unconditional group model, reverse the interaction, or add the absolute conditional observation score. Inspect the true-category versus strongest wrong-category interaction margin after all rankings have been formed, separately for successes and errors of the frozen base. The desired outcome is an improvement above the strong base and above the matched controls, plus evidence of positive margins on actual errors.

Conditioning a zero-shot score on the natural background likelihood has precedent in language modeling. The proposed transfer here concerns a *change of resolution in one image* and a within-photo association measured in an unlabelled target domain. Whole-image scoring, named domain prompts and the original photo index still come from the existing lexical system.
