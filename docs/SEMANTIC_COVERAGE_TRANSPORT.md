# The cost of losing relevant gallery coverage under a rank change

On the full exploratory Sketchy Ext-2 split the previous expected-AP selector increased the number of correct top-one matches by 81, but 464 modified queries lost an average of 17.18 relevant images in their first 200 results. Their mean AP dropped 10.49 percentage points. The 1,115 queries that gained relevant images gained 8.24 images and 2.54 AP points on average. This motivates measuring how much coverage a proposed change can destroy for a still-plausible interpretation of a query. The diagnosis used category labels after inference. [Measured failure on previously locked predictions](https://github.com/HieuNM1804/training-free-ZS-SBIR/blob/e558058/tf_sbir/latent_intent_diagnosis.py)

Use 64 spherical clusters in centered frozen gallery RGB embeddings to represent unlabeled visual populations. For population g and ranking π, let r(g,j,π) be the position of the j-th member of that population. A reader can accept a different photograph of the same subject. The j-th encounter is therefore matched with the j-th encounter when comparing two rankings. Take all encounter quantiles within every group. The baseline is the previously frozen word, RGB gallery and regional retriever. Candidate rankings come from the original visual cosine, the eight most plausible public names, and the soft average of sixteen names. Each proposal uses the same list of mixing strengths. The baseline is always available.

Let c be an increasing inspection cost. The signed benefit for a population is the mean of c(old rank) − c(new rank). Its downside is the mean positive part of c(new rank) − c(old rank). Matching corresponding quantiles minimizes that downside over all couplings with the empirical marginals. Indeed the expected positive part equals half the sum of the expected absolute displacement and the fixed signed displacement. Monotone coupling attains the one-dimensional Wasserstein distance for the absolute term. If two rankings exchange the identities of photos within the same group while preserving all encounter ranks, this measure assigns zero delay. An identity-based displacement comparator penalizes the exchange. The signed gain is identical for both couplings, which isolates the effect of downside matching.

Estimate two distributions over populations using only the current sketch and unlabeled gallery. One comes from the sketch's native cosine to photos. The other marginalizes the gallery evidence of the sketch's sixteen nominated public names. Both use group means to give each population equal prior mass before visual evidence. For each posterior compute expected signed gain less a nonnegative multiple of expected delay. Choose the ranking with the greatest lower endpoint. This is the exact worst case for every convex mixture of the two distributions because utility is linear in the distribution. The resulting guarantee concerns these modeled populations and cost functions. Actual class relevance and accuracy require empirical verification.

We use inspection costs with derivative r^(-1−γ), where γ is 0, 0.5 or 1, and compare downside penalties of 0, 0.25, 1 and 4. The method has no precision cutoff or dataset identifier. Matched controls use ordinary expected gain, identity-based displacement, permuted groups preserving all group sizes, one posterior, and the mean of the two posteriors. Fixed blends, unpenalized selectors and controls receive independent tuning. Every pilot action is scored using the original KD-SBIR metrics for the complete photo gallery before aggregating outcomes for each policy. Record losses of the last relevant hit, number of queries on which precision falls, and the oracle over the *identical* action set. The oracle is a diagnosis using ground truth. The primary uses the semantic lower bound over both posteriors and a positive downside penalty. Success calls for measurable gains over the undisturbed ranker and the optimized simpler controls.

The risk-sensitive retrieval precedent penalizes losses relative to a baseline across labeled queries. This study measures unavoidable delays within possible *unlabeled* interpretations of a single query by matching the discovery ranks of interchangeable photos. The frozen word bridge and RGB neighbourhood operator are inherited. The experiment distinguishes this proposed contribution from novelty of risk-sensitive ranking itself.
