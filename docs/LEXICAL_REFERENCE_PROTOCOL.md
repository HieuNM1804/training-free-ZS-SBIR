# Prior from unlabeled gallery choices

## Hypothesis

Sensitivity to vocabulary multiplicity can enter through the unit of prior mass. The independent-softmax baseline gives every spelling one unit. The first correlated-choice study changes the entire posterior for each image. It sharpens individual choices substantially on Sketchy Ext-1. This follow-up uses the same random utilities only to estimate the distribution of lexical meanings in the unlabeled target domain, then restores the original likelihood of each photo.

For each gallery photo and random draw, select the word maximizing its frozen photo-prompt CLIP logit plus correlated Gumbel noise. Count winners across the entire gallery and normalize to obtain a measure $\mu$. For each individual photo $j$, compute $p(w\mid j)\propto \mu_w\exp(z_{jw})$. Marginalize the unmodified sketch likelihood against these probabilities and apply the original RGB diffusion. When two names have identical text directions in both domains, appending copies leaves the total mass of their class, the photo normalization, the sketch readout, and hence the ranking invariant, provided ties use a consistent representative. Inference requires only the unlabeled gallery; other queries play no role. The gallery measure is estimated once. The proposal does not require language-model synonym annotation.

## Predeclared tests

The primary estimator and 256 draws use the same seed and constants as the first choice study. Select no alternative by its transfer score. The fixed estimator is compared against a fitted global mixture prior, the average ordinary gallery posterior (one EM update), the average concentration-matched posterior, hard-winner frequencies, and a geometry permutation preserving the set of CLIP text directions. A second independent random seed and a deterministic half-weight for both members of each tight mutual-nearest pair test numerical sensitivity and simple redundancy correction. Recompute all affected probabilities and priors after removing one term from each pair. Compare top-1 change rate and top-100 retention on identical queries for the proposed and fitted-mixture priors. As in the original study, these pairs are CLIP-near terms and are not human-certified synonyms.

For a retrieval contribution, its gain must survive the strongest simple prior matched on the same gallery and graph. For a robustness contribution, changes under distinct near-duplicate names must shrink without a large utility loss, and must be larger than the sampling variation of the prior. If wrong geometry or the hard/temperature prior explains the result, the study supplies a diagnostic, not evidence for this correlated estimator. Use the original KD evaluator for every query on Sketchy Ext-1, Ext-2 and 30-class TU-Berlin. Store the Sketchy results before transfer. All three splits have historical evaluations of other methods.

The three direct mechanism sources for the branch are the vocabulary bridge in [FreeDom](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/WACV2025/html/Efthymiadis_Composed_Image_Retrieval_for_Training-Free_Domain_Conversion_WACV_2025_paper.html), correlated class utilities in [Collier et al.](https://research.google/pubs/correlated-input-dependent-label-noise-in-large-scale-image-classification/), and the gallery operator of [Wang et al.](https://pages.ucsd.edu/~ztu/publication/cvpr12_CrDP.pdf). Bayes normalization and marginalization are standard. The specific empirical reference and the intervention are hypotheses to test.
