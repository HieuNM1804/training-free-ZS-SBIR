# Can a small population of gallery photographs explain all the clues in one sketch?

Combining two already-aggregated retrieval scores gave little class-level benefit on real misses, even with an oracle revealing the category of every photo. We instead consider separate named clues. A sketch can suggest several names referring to one subject or to different aspects of the depicted object. A possible population of photo witnesses must collectively account for the nominated clues. It matters whether the clues habitually occur in the same photographs or in complementary examples. Merely registering the frequency of each clue cannot distinguish these cases.

The frozen query nominates six or eight public names. For each nominated name in the target gallery, count a photo as supporting the clue when its CLIP similarity is strictly above a fixed gallery quantile of .90 or .97. These are exploratory operating points, common to all names and datasets, and ties at the threshold are treated consistently. An image then has a small binary support mask. A catalog of 24 or 64 target-only spherical visual clusters estimates a distribution of support masks for each possible population. Clusters are indexed without sketch access or category labels. For each cluster compute the probability that r independent photo draws together cover all the sketch's nominated clues. For r=1 the probability requires all clues in one photo. We also evaluate r=2 and 4. Compute the exact value by inclusion–exclusion from its subset cumulative distribution. Repeated photo IDs are allowed by this with-replacement model. Six or eight labels per query allow all subsets to be enumerated.

If two clues are each present half the time, the two-photo cover probability is 3/4 when both clues always occur together and 1/2 when they always occur on different photos. An independent-marginal model gives 9/16 in both cases. Which difference is helpful for real retrieval is empirical. Raise the exact probability to the power 1/k to express it in per-clue units. Compare it with the probability predicted by independent clue marginals, their difference, arithmetic mean support, a single witness and an independent cyclic reassignment of every clue among photographs within each cluster. That reassignment preserves the exact empirical frequency of every clue in every cluster. An unlabeled group correction reorders the strong regional ranker across the complete original gallery. All scoring queries remain independent.

Separately, with the actual retrieval already scored, repeat the witness calculation using the true categories as populations. It measures whether the mechanism could help if recognizing the target classes were solved. No oracle rank enters retrieval or parameter selection. Choose only with equal mean mAP on the two exploratory Sketchy unseen splits. Test the premise against the separately tuned simpler controls before interpreting any rise over the original ranker.

The Boolean union of observations and their explanatory combination are established in the discrete basis problem. This retrieval hypothesis uses the distribution of many alternative covers for a particular independent query, its identifiable marginal-preserving counterfactual and the actual rank changes they produce. This study uses three direct research mechanisms in total.
