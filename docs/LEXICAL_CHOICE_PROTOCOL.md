# Changing the granularity of a public vocabulary in cross-domain retrieval

## Identifiable defect and proposed remedy

Under the frozen word-matching baseline, repeating one latent concept under multiple names multiplies its unnormalized mass. This can change the ordering of photos even if every copy is observationally identical in both domains. Diffusing the scores through a fixed image graph preserves the dependence in general. The repository already removes duplicate strings. The operational question is how much the remaining vocabulary depends on distinct terms that encode similarly.

Describe the baseline photo softmax as the winning probabilities in an independent Gumbel race. Replace independent errors across names with a random utility field defined by the frozen photo-prompt geometry. For each draw use a vector of independent standard Normal variables in the text embedding space. Its dot product with every unit word vector gives jointly Gaussian variables with those words' Gram matrix as correlation. Map each variable through its own Gaussian CDF and the standard Gumbel quantile. Each marginal error remains standard Gumbel. Add these errors to the original CLIP logits and take the winning term. Average the original sketch likelihood of the winning terms, then apply the original RGB diffusion operator. This design uses 256 draws and the checkpoint logit scale. The draws and vocabulary are shared through the evaluation, with a second random seed as a numerical sensitivity control.

Exact copies of both domain embeddings have identical utilities under every draw and yield identical sketch likelihoods. Copy insertion therefore leaves every score unchanged for fixed randomness and a fixed geometry, with consistent handling of ties. Cosine-close text directions share perturbations, but actual synonyms need not have identical CLIP logits. Their stability is an empirical question. The geometry and the random directions are independent of target labels and of the other queries.

## Falsification

1. On the natural vocabulary, compare baseline diffusion, fixed correlated choice, plain hard assignment, identical-budget independently sampled softmax, permutation of geometry across word identities, and a softmax whose average probability of two identical choices is matched on unlabeled gallery photos. The matched statistic controls for sharpening. Evaluate a second set of 256 draws without using it to select a seed.
2. Use the same random draws with an augmented vocabulary in a physical copy-insertion check. Also evaluate the exact multiplicity perturbation of the baseline across all queries, repeating a fixed random eighth of the original names eightfold. Removing exact duplicate strings solves this artificial control by itself.
3. Find mutual nearest words by average cosine of the two prompt banks. Retain up to 1,024 disjoint pairs with both cosines at least 0.92 and drop the lexicographically later term. The intervention uses two actual distinct names. Compare changes to top-1, top-100 overlap and mAP between the original and thinned vocabulary for each scorer. Similarity is measured by CLIP and does not certify synonymy.
4. The configuration and all controls are fixed before collecting scores. Evaluate every sketch against every photo under KD-SBIR. Summarize uncertainty by class bootstrap. An advantage matched by a change of temperature or by shuffling the word geometry weakens the case for geometry in the retrieval score. Stability confined to artificial copies supports only the structural guarantee.

## Closest prior work

Collier et al. model correlation between class utilities in image classification with a trained, input-dependent Normal model. Here the text geometry specifies the dependence analytically, the marginal error law matches the baseline, and the object being ranked is a photo. The word bridge and the gallery operator are from FreeDom and Wang et al. These are the three direct mechanism sources for this experiment. [CVPR 2021](https://research.google/pubs/correlated-input-dependent-label-noise-in-large-scale-image-classification/) · [WACV 2025](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/WACV2025/html/Efthymiadis_Composed_Image_Retrieval_for_Training-Free_Domain_Conversion_WACV_2025_paper.html) · [CVPR 2012](https://pages.ucsd.edu/~ztu/publication/cvpr12_CrDP.pdf)

SynCLIP investigates inconsistency under synonymous expressions in dense prediction by modifying pretraining. Fan et al. discuss vocabulary curation for localization. These papers preclude a broad claim that synonym sensitivity in CLIP is newly discovered. The narrower proposition concerns aggregation over aliases in a hidden public vocabulary used to rank two images. [SynCLIP](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2026/html/Xie_SynCLIP_Synonym-Coherent_Language-Image_Pretraining_for_Robust_Open-Vocabulary_Dense_Perception_CVPR_2026_paper.html) · [Fan et al.](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2023/papers/Fan_Unsupervised_Open-Vocabulary_Object_Localization_in_Videos_ICCV_2023_paper.pdf)
