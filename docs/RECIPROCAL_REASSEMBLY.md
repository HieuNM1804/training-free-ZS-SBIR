# Does each image keep its meaning when rendered in the other image's arrangement?

Zoomed sketch regions were useful, while matching outer and inner word distributions primarily changed the marginal representation. A correspondence between two individual images offers a stronger test. If a sketch and a photo depict the same kind of object, arranging the original sketch patches in the photo's pose and arranging the original photo patches in the sketch's pose may preserve semantic agreement. Matching patches well by cosine alone is an alternative explanation.

Find a one-to-one assignment of the 49 sketch patches to the 49 photo locations using frozen, gallery-position-centered intermediate CLIP descriptors. Keep every original 32 by 32 patch. The maximum-weight assignment uses no image contours, segmentation, pixels from another query or surrogate sketch generator. Let $Q_P$ denote the sketch patches in the photo's arrangement and $P_Q$ the reverse arrangement of the photo patches. Re-encode both with the original 12 visual blocks.

With an affinity $S$, form $I=\tfrac12[S(Q_P,P)+S(Q,P_Q)-S(Q,P)-S(Q_P,P_Q)]$. Terms added to the score by just one image cancel. Identical arrangements give zero. For dot products this is one half of the dot product between the change of the sketch vector and the opposite change of the photo vector. For a lexical affinity use log overlap over source- and target-prompt readings. Evaluate the same expression after conjugating the estimated permutation by a rotation and a reflection of the square. These controls keep its displacement lengths and cycle structure. The proposed score is the observed interaction minus its mean under those two controlled misalignments. The controls are two specified transforms, with no assumption that their average is a complete null distribution.

For a tractable candidate stage, cluster unlabelled gallery photos into 64 spherical groups. Select one actual photo closest to each group center and nominate six groups for each sketch using the regional baseline. Apply each representative's score correction to its group's images while preserving the sum of evidence on the short list. Use the same six groups and representatives in all comparisons. Test the raw interaction, the permutation controls, assignment cost without re-encoding, and a one-sided reconstruction score. Measure whether the correct group was represented as a diagnostic using labels after retrieval. If the underlying support is insufficient it limits attainable gains.

Three direct sources of mechanisms are the public word bridge, the gallery operator and optimal-transport semantic correspondence. This exact assignment is a discrete, equal-mass transport. The part proposed here is comparing the four *re-encoded* layouts, including the opposite-pose condition, relative to geometry-preserving misalignments. Use Sketchy as development, comparing the same complete galleries. Only a promising mechanism proceeds to a full Sketchy lock and TU-Berlin.
