# Full one-observation test

The pilot found modest gains over a fixed gallery-selected region and a strong preference for large regions. The full follow-up checks whether choosing a precise location has value once scale is controlled. All deployable single-region methods receive one global sketch and one additional native, unreflected zoom. Every photo stays in the gallery. This is an action-budget experiment. Timings from retrospective evaluation of every possible action do not measure online latency.

Choose among the best three held-out pair-risk settings by the mean of the two full Sketchy mAP values, breaking ties by mean precision. Independently shortlist the best two within-panel risk and Shannon information settings and the strongest fixed-gallery choice. Lock all settings before measuring TU. The primary check compares the selected action against choosing the best gallery-wide location at exactly its proposed scale and against the exact expectation over uniform locations of that same scale. The matched classical entropy and within-panel decision rules use the same temperature, number of rivals, lexical readout and scoring coefficient. The full-image retriever, uniform random single zoom, full 13-region pooling, and an evaluation-only one-region oracle indicate the cost and remaining headroom. A bootstrap over classes quantifies paired differences. The artifact retains the actual official AP and precision for every single action for the primary readout, enabling separate analyses for initial hits and misses.

The full evaluator replays each possible single-action rank list and each global or pooled rank list through the original KD-SBIR function. A deterministic policy selects that action's original per-query result. Stochastic and oracle rows aggregate the individual original results. These rows have no single deployable ranking. The data-dependent diagnostic using the oracle never enters action selection. The local word resources, domains, pretraining and gallery use are identical to the original pilot.
