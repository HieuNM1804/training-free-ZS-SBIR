# Do interactions between parts of real images distinguish categories across depictions?

This is an explanatory measurement of the frozen visual encoder before deciding whether the proposed research direction warrants a retrieval algorithm. The hypothesis is that categories misidentified across depictions retain stronger visual information in the joint response to complementary parts than in the sum of the two marginal responses. A model having nonzero interactions is insufficient. Those interactions must carry the relevant identity across domains and across independent reference images.

## Data and controls fixed before measurement

Use only the two exploratory Sketchy splits. For each listed class, sample 32 distinct original photos with at least two redraws and choose two human drawings of each, using seed 42. Use an independent deterministic seed per class so shared categories have the same subjects. Draw both reference panels from other photos of the split, excluding all source identities, and keep the panels disjoint. For each of four random balanced partitions of the native 49 patch positions, each panel provides two different reference photos. Keep the four masks fixed across all images. The evidence for transfer to new subjects comes from category-matched photos of different identities. The evidence for source identity comes from within-class choices. Ties count for one half. Report the sensitivity to removing all four shared classes from both splits. These are related development samples and the figures have no independent cross-domain validation claim.

Let F be the pre-final-normalization visual CLS token with its feature mean removed, followed by the fixed CLIP gain and visual projection. This gives a vector in the usual 512 coordinates before dividing by any sample-dependent length. Write x=(xA,xB) and draw two references u and v. The measured four-condition contrast is I(x)=F(x)−F(xA,uB)−F(vA,xB)+F(vA,uB). Its additive complement is L(x)=F(x)−I(x). If the encoder is additive over the two groups, I is exactly zero on any inputs. The product distribution refers to pairs of independently sampled group donors. This identity establishes cancellation of additive effects. Whether the surviving component carries meaning is tested on real observations.

Average the four fixed mask measurements within each panel. For the cross-domain diagnostic, compare sketches read under one panel with photos read under the other and average the two orientations. Analyze each real redraw independently. For two sampled sketches a and b and two photos A and B, measure the sign of (a−b)·(A−B). It asks which photo belongs with each drawing. Any constant offset of either domain cancels. For category choices, select sketches from distinct classes and use photos of a different subject of the same class, using a fixed cyclic half-sample shift. Every pair of distinct classes has equal weight. Within-class choices use two distinct subjects and their actual source photos. Also check agreement between actual redraws, photo-only category discrimination, and a 601-word semantic control from the frozen cached CLIP features. Report item-level descriptive rescues and damage relative to the semantic control. Keep the pairing fixed for each method. For between-class differences, bootstrap classes as vertices of the pair table, retain multiplicities and omit edges connecting the same original class (10,000 draws, seed 42). For within-class differences, bootstrap class means. No full-gallery ranking, novel retrieval pipeline or model tuning is part of this test.

The fixed primary gate for a subsequent method is a positive interaction-minus-additive category-assignment effect on both Sketchy splits with class-bootstrap lower bounds above zero, positive net correct choices against the semantic control, and consistent direction after omitting shared classes. Failure is sufficient to reject this four-condition proposal for development as a principal contribution. Passing a probe leaves the full mAP target and the novelty claim open.

## Closest precedents

Functional ANOVA already identifies interaction terms through removal of marginal effects. Shapley–Taylor already attributes interactions to subsets by finite differences. CutMix inserts regions from other real images in training. A new application of the four-condition subtraction by itself would have a weak methodological novelty claim. The question at issue is whether these operations reveal an identifiable, cross-domain loss mechanism on actual images, beyond an ordinary change in feature scale or an additive effect.
