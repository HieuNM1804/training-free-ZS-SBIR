# Coupling two explanations without altering either marginal distribution

Consider one unlabeled gallery index, built with the **same literal public words for the two image domains** and the frozen CLIP scale. No domain labels enter the scorer. Let $b=B_i$ be an image's lexical profile, $l$ the query evidence, and $h(v)$ the mean query evidence of *other* gallery images with weight proportional to their support for $v$. The previous domain-neutral study found independent explanatory words superior to a single shared word on both permitted datasets. This is a post-hypothesis experiment. Its primary rule is fixed from the following sampling construction before this study's own results, including the transfer score.

Take two independent word samples for reading the query and two independent word samples for reading the gallery feedback, all drawn from $b$. Of the two bijections between the pairs, choose the one with fewer exact word-identity collisions. Resolve a tie equiprobably. Then draw one of its two matched pairs uniformly. Every occurrence in each original sample is matched once, so the two word marginals are **exactly** $b$. This is a two-by-two discrete assignment using an equality cost. It gently reduces repeated evidence while retaining the possibility of an identical word when both mini-samples make this unavoidable. It models two lexical identities, whose meanings can still be close.

Let $c=\sum_w b_w^2$. The expected ordered-pair probabilities are
$$J_{wv}=b_wb_v(1-c+b_w+b_v-b_wb_v)-\mathbf{1}_{w=v}\,b_w^2(1-c).$$
For different $w,v$, compare the two matchings conditioned on the first ordered pair being $(w,v)$. The difference between the probability that the original matching wins and the probability that the swap wins is $b_w+b_v-b_wb_v-c$. For identical $w=v$, the original matching loses precisely when both other sampled words differ from $w$. Multiplication by $b_w b_v$ yields the displayed formula. Both $J$ marginals sum to $b$. The total probability of a repeat falls by $\sum_w b_w^2(1-b_w)^2$. All terms are observable on the unlabeled gallery.

The main score is $\sum_{wv}J_{wv}l_w h(v)$. The matrix is diagonal plus four rank-one terms, so all 20,931 words are integrated exactly without sampling word pairs or materializing a vocabulary-squared matrix per photo. A novel part of this proposal is the elimination of that assignment inside a query-dependent gallery verification score. The primary uses no strength parameter, seed, source prompts or target prompts. The same low-rank expression yields exact single-word counterfactual score margins, including changes in all gallery feedback. The code checks this against physical interventions on every word of a real query for each split.

## Falsification

For two equally probable words, independent draws repeat with probability 1/2. The minimum-collision coupling repeats with probability 3/8, and each of the two off-diagonal pairs has probability 5/16. If the two channels support opposite words perfectly, their joint score rises from 1/4 to 5/16. If they support the same word perfectly, it falls from 1/4 to 3/16. The change is completely determined by the observed evidence.

1. Pair words independently, while keeping the same marginals, score functions, gallery, and witness-image exclusion.
2. Reverse the assignment objective to **maximize** identity collisions. It has the same marginals. When score gain follows fewer collisions rather than simply more feedback, primary should improve upon both independent and reverse couplings. A tiny gain only identifies a small effect.
3. Completely reject independent pairs with identical words. This changes the individual marginals to $b_w(1-b_w)/(1-c)$. Construct the independent product of *these altered marginals* to isolate the contribution of the pair constraint. Compare the matched control directly. Complete rejection is the size-two diagonal k-DPP case and measures a different distribution over pairs.
4. Compare with a single shared word, collision-conditioned independent draws, and the linear baseline. Verify the exact assignment matrix and marginals against all four-draw outcomes from small real distributions. Replay the KD evaluator on every query, record class-bootstrap intervals, and freeze the same primary before TU-Berlin. All existing scores are exploratory on the same permitted data.
