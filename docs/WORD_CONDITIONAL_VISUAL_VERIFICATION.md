# Does native visual agreement survive a counterfactual match on public words?

The locked regional word system occasionally suppresses a correct visual result. On Sketchy Ext-2, 1,531 sketches had native AP at least five points above the regional base. Average AP in that selected set fell from 25.92 to 2.05 percent when switching to words. On Ext-1 and TU, selecting either entire rank list with knowledge of the true labels had at most 0.05 and 0.13 points of mean AP headroom. This motivates revising individual candidate scores and controlling for the visual information already conveyed by words. [Original measurements](https://github.com/HieuNM1804/training-free-ZS-SBIR/blob/b53d3f4/docs/results/gallery-directed-region-acquisition/native-region-disagreement.json)

Index each unlabelled target photo using two views from the same frozen CLIP. A is its global image. B is a unit-normalized mean of its region embeddings at the two existing scales. Split target photos into groups using their compact public-word distributions in Hellinger space. These groups are estimates of linguistic similarity. They need not coincide with benchmark classes.

One hypothesis says that, conditional on the indicated word group, a sketch and this candidate express a common residual visual content. The alternative says they are independent draws of the residual. Estimate the paired visual covariances from two renditions of the same unlabelled photo after centering each view within its word group. Whiten the two marginals and diagonalize their cross-covariance. Estimate the strength of each resulting correlation on different photographs. Split the gallery into four folds. Each candidate is evaluated with a basis estimated from two other folds and correlations calibrated on the third. The query is never used to fit anything. Group assignments are built once from the unlabelled gallery. The same-group condition is supplied separately for each candidate.

For standardized canonical coordinates x and y and a held-out correlation r, use the Gaussian log likelihood ratio between a correlated and an independent pair. Its contribution is minus one half log(1-r²), plus [rxy − r²(x²+y²)/2]/(1-r²). Average across the two possible orders of the source and target renditions. The quadratic terms can penalize a match whose visual deviations have incompatible magnitudes, even when their dot product is large. Frozen CLIP is not guaranteed to follow this Gaussian model, and same-photo repeatability can encode nuisance appearance rather than true category. Test these assumptions.

Add the resulting score to the locked regional word score. Standardize both over the same unlabelled photo gallery per independent query. Compare fixed fusion with raw global cosine, equally expensive cross-view cosine, gallery-centered cross-view cosine, a full joint verifier without word conditioning, and conditioning on permuted word groups with identical sizes. Keep the same covariance basis and switch off the quadratic terms, or evaluate how much a within-word reordering alone changes retrieval. Log held-out view agreement, false same-word verification, per-query rankings and regressions on actual mistakes. Compare the selected primary with separately optimized controls and with controls matched at its settings.

Development uses the two full unseen Sketchy splits after a balanced screen. Freeze before scoring TU-Berlin. The public names and the domain prompts are external resources. The same method could be used in other domains with analogous views, with transfer evidence limited to sketch → photo here. Three direct sources are public lexical retrieval, the existing RGB gallery operator and joint Bayesian verification. The discriminative hypothesis here is the word-conditional, gallery-only counterfactual tested against an actual same-photo rendering.
