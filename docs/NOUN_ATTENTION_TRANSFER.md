# Does the final CLIP text block change how much it reads an object name or what it reads from it?

On the balanced Sketchy pilot the most promising contextual name intervention was at the input to the very last text block. A name can receive more attention under “photo” and yield a different message under “sketch”. These effects are inseparable if the word-level endpoint is the only measurement. The present hypothesis is that one path carries depiction-dependent bias, while the other can retain a correct subject cue. Which path and sign improve retrieval is an empirical question.

For a public name, align its identical BPE tokens between each pair of existing source and target prompts. For any fixed background and EOT head, let m be the total attention probability assigned to these noun tokens, v their conditional mean value, and u the conditional mean value of the other tokens. The actual attended vector is u + m(v − u). Swapping just the noun states before the last block preserves the EOT query and all non-noun keys and values. It can change m and v independently. For a name with multiple BPE pieces, v includes how the head weights the pieces relative to one another.

Compute four forward readouts with the noun masses and conditional messages from either depiction. Pass each recombination through the original head output projection, residual connection, MLP, final normalization and text projection. Average the two original sentence templates and normalize within each recombination. The mass contribution is its change holding each of the two messages fixed, averaged over both. The conditional-message contribution is the corresponding difference holding each mass fixed. Repeat with both original caption backgrounds and average. These two contributions sum to the symmetric contextual noun effect. Measure their numerical closure, agreement of true endpoint combinations with the native text block, and discrepancy from the archived noun intervention. All public name measurements are independent of retrieval labels, sketches and gallery content.

Use the two directions to score the current sketch globally and regionally with the same frozen gallery index. Give the two paths separately selected coefficients. Equal coefficients reduce to the original unsplit noun effect. Controls include a common change of noun log odds across all heads, permutation of those changes between heads, a collinear component with the same projection onto the full noun effect, whole-prompt extrapolation, and an unsplit noun change with its own gain. All controls see the same galleries, and their parameters may be tuned independently on both unseen Sketchy splits. An improvement from changing noun attention alone must survive the optimized simpler controls and transfer to TU-Berlin before attributing retrieval gains to a particular path.

The separation of attention mass and message follows the definition of attention. The input-state exchange in the two natural depictions adapts the causal localization methodology of Basu and colleagues. The proposed retrieval mechanism concerns applying separate changes to the two measured paths, with a symmetric decomposition that preserves the original name intervention. Related analyses have already shown that attention weights by themselves can poorly explain a transformer's output.
