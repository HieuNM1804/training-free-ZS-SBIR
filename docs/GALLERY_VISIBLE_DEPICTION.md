# Which depiction distinctions can change what the gallery sees?

The two prompted versions of “a sketch of X” and “a photo of X” sometimes rank the names of a sketch differently. On full exploratory Sketchy, enhancing that difference in a query's observed head directions improved both mAP scores. Direct textual extrapolation improved one split. This motivates testing which component of the difference is visible to the target images. The experiment below acts on the public word evidence of a frozen global and regional retriever. It uses the ordinary frozen CLIP embeddings of each view. [Preceding sign test](https://github.com/HieuNM1804/training-free-ZS-SBIR/blob/ea8c50f/docs/PAIRED_PROMPT_FULL_FOLLOWUP.md)

Let B(i,w) be the public word weight of photo i in the fixed RGB gallery index. For a query view v, a(w) is its native sketch-prompt CLIP logit and d(w) is its sketch-prompt logit minus the corresponding photo-prompt logit. The retriever gives the joint distribution P(i,w|v) proportional to exp(a(w)) B(i,w). Center d under the word marginal of this distribution. Then h(i) = E[d(W)|i,v] is exactly the derivative of the normalized log likelihood of photo i when adding a small multiple of d to the word logits. Return that image effect to word space through t(w) = E[h(I)|w,v]. All sums use every photo and every word present in the relevant index.

Three identities make the hypothesis testable. If two names produce identical distributions over gallery photos, an opposing contrast between them with zero weighted mean contributes nothing to the round trip. If the names yield disjoint photo distributions, the round trip returns the entire contrast. In general the inner product of d and t under the word marginal equals the variance of h over photos. Thus the scalar b = Var(h)/Var(d) lies between zero and one. The component t − bd is orthogonal to d. This last term distinguishes changing which concepts are emphasized from simply changing the strength of the original prompt effect.

The proposed correction is a + αt + β(d−t). We choose α and β on Sketchy. Each view preserves the total likelihood it originally gave to the gallery, preventing one crop's influence from increasing merely by adding the same constant to all its word logits. Use the fixed global word/RGB index and the existing candidate-conditioned readout over 13 regions in each of two poses. The same rule can operate on the global view, on all local views, or on both. Every decision depends only on the current query and the unlabeled photo gallery.

Compare against replacing t by its matched scalar projection bd, against a word-specific shuffle of target image identities that preserves each word's marginal and histogram, and against direct signed prompt extrapolation with independent strength selection. Direct extrapolation is tested both with and without the constraint on each view's total likelihood. Each control can select its support and parameters independently. The test measures a signed depiction shift. Earlier word-to-word translation in this repository diffused the query likelihood itself. The transfer operator and its general use for word translation are established prior work. The claim under study concerns estimating a nuisance or useful contrast through its measurable effect on the actual target likelihood, and whether changing its observable and remaining parts differently improves retrieval.

Use the original balanced Sketchy samples for a pilot with the complete galleries, recording the exact original KD-SBIR results for the chosen conditions. A full evaluation, when warranted, selects by equal-weight unseen Sketchy mAP then precision and freezes before measuring all TU-Berlin classes. Historical transfer results have already been observed. Target labels can measure errors but do not enter the operator or its selection.
