# Can a photograph reproduce which semantic concepts are supported by the same sketch regions?

A bag of word scores does not encode whether two concepts arise together within an image. Two images can share every word's distribution over local views and still differ in whether those words belong to the same regions. We test this directly on errors of the locked public-word regional retriever. Each query nominates its top public names from the unchanged CLIP global view. No class name or other query is available to the scorer. No region alignment between images is assumed.

Use the existing thirteen regions in two orientations for both inputs. Keep the two region sizes separate. Within one image, compare every unordered pair of regions at each size and orientation. For name a, let its response in a region be the dot product of its domain-prompt embedding with that region's frozen CLIP embedding after projecting away the native full-image embedding. The query uses source-domain prompts and the photo uses target-domain prompts. The average product of the signs of the changes for a and b is Kendall concordance (tau-a). Compute it separately in each scale and orientation, then average the four equally. Ties contribute zero. With the nominated names and native direction fixed, a common permutation of regions and a strictly increasing recalibration of the projected responses for any individual word preserve this measurement.

Even meaningless rotations of the same region differences make nearby CLIP words tend to move together. Conditional on the native image embedding z, project the two text embeddings onto the tangent space normal to z and normalize them. If their cosine is c, a uniformly rotated nonzero region difference in that tangent space agrees in sign on the two names with probability 1/2 + arcsin(c)/pi. Subtract the corresponding expected sign product 2 arcsin(c)/pi. This reference preserves the image's native alignment with each name, the size of each tangential change, and the arrangement between region differences under a shared rotation. An exactly zero change contributes zero in both the measured and expected signatures. This is an expectation over rotations, not a model of independent crops.

Project the resulting off-diagonal matrix onto the complement of additive effects of individual names. For K names, let m denote its mean over distinct ordered pairs and let r_a denote the mean for row a over b distinct from a. Set h_a = (K−1)(r_a−m)/(K−2). The residual for (a,b) is R_ab−m−h_a−h_b. Every off-diagonal row sums to zero and every contribution of the form c+s_a+s_b vanishes. Take the inner product of the sketch and candidate residuals over named pairs. Under independent uniform rotations the expected unnormalized product is zero by linearity. Also evaluate cosine as a scale-normalized alternative. The complete ranking uses this image-pair evidence with the fixed regional lexical retrieval score. It remains possible that an inaccurate object hypothesis offers a more similar dependence pattern than the right category.

Tests use native scores, complete sorted per-word regional distributions, ordinary Pearson correlations, raw sign concordance, language geometry alone, the single-name additive effects, and the same residual without the analytic subtraction. The central control permutes regional observations separately for each name, strictly within scale and identically in both orientations. It preserves the empirical distribution for every word, its mean, variance, and paired response under reflection. Independent permutations in the two domains remove common semantic concurrence. A shared region permutation must leave the real descriptor invariant. Report all these checks. Run first on true and false candidates competing in the existing retrieval, then on complete galleries with the original KD-SBIR evaluator. Reusing the source-photo identity or category for choosing gallery support or tuning on TU-Berlin is outside the method.

Rank-based dependence estimators under unknown monotone transformations have been studied extensively. Computing image descriptors by pooling pairwise channel interactions is also established. The proposition under test here is more specific: after accounting for the frozen language geometry and additive single-name effects, the named interactions induced by viewing two different images may predict cross-domain relevance. Its merit depends on defeating controls that preserve the per-name marginals. The three direct mechanisms are rank dependence, a lexical bridge and the inherited RGB gallery operator. Bilinear pooling is a comparator.
