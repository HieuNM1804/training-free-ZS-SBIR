# Infer a public scene concept from an internal spatial configuration

GCWE currently transfers words after scoring individual sketch–photo patch correspondences. Here the latent variable is a public scene concept, and what must transfer is a distribution of geometric observations under that concept across different photos. Independently in every image, use the 49 native contextual patch tokens of frozen OpenAI CLIP ViT-B/32. Choose an anchor patch uniformly, order all 48 other patches by their similarity to it, choose k uniformly in 1…24 and read the squared grid distances from the anchor to the patches of ranks k and 49−k. The observation is the **joint pair of distances**. Averaging exact counts over anchor and k gives H for the whole image. It is invariant to any anchor-wise strictly increasing transform of patch similarity, and to an isometry of the square grid with token identities carried along. CLIP's encoder itself need not be equivariant to rotating the input. No orientation, threshold, edges, part annotations or additional encoder are used.

For word W, estimate the distribution of this geometric event by mixing each reference photo's H with its frozen photo-prompt probability of W. Add one pseudo-image drawn from the distribution of two uniformly chosen distinct non-anchor locations on the grid. The same prior is used for every word. The empirical probability of W in the reference gallery controls how strongly its geometric distribution departs from that background. Reference photos and scored photos are assigned to opposite seed-42 folds. Sketches are always processed independently.

Start from the original whole-sketch word probabilities. For a measured event e the posterior for W is proportional to its prior times P(e|W). The primary update averages these posteriors under the sketch's actual H, integrating the uncertainty about which local configuration expresses its scene meaning. A second readout imposes a shared concept across m geometric observations. Use the mean conditional log likelihood over the sketch and the expected sufficient counts for m=1,4,16 observations. This last plug-in uses expected log evidence; it is distinct from averaging exact posterior draws. Rank every held-fold image by its agreement with the inferred scene distribution, using all 20,931 words.

Primary controls retain the same photo pool, CLIP word priors and distance histograms. (1) Use the two one-distance marginals for each scene word under the independence assumption. (2) Permute both endpoint identities by a uniform random patch permutation independently of visual affinity, averaged analytically. Its distribution is the same grid background for every image, so posterior and baseline are identical. (3) Compare against direct word agreement. An improvement over direct scores alone would not identify joint geometric information. A promising readout must improve mAP over both direct agreement and the matched independent-distance model on both Sketchy splits. Use the same full official galleries and seeded pilot query IDs as the semantic studies. If eligible, freeze by mean mAP then mean precision before scoring the remaining queries and TU-Berlin. Report all prespecified alternatives.

Local self-similarity has been used for cross-domain matching including sketch detection. The earlier internal universal-self-similarity experiment also used a gallery graph. The proposed job for geometry here is to infer the scene-word posterior from a class-conditional joint law, rather than match two visual descriptors. Existing work on visual constellations already models statistics of parts, which limits how much novelty can be claimed for a geometric co-occurrence alone.
