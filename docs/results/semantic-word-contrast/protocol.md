# Cross-domain semantic retrieval with identifiable word contrasts

The global word-score baseline sums p(w|sketch) b(w|photo) across the public vocabulary. For two related words i,j, that sum within the pair equals one half of the product of the two pair masses plus one half of the product of the two signed contrasts. The coarse-mass term asks whether the two images activate the same semantic neighborhood. The contrast term asks whether the side of a specific distinction preferred by one domain is also preferred by the other.

Build disjoint pairs from reciprocal nearest neighbors in the average of the two normalized domain-prompt text banks. Leave other words unchanged. For each pair, the corresponding CLIP log odds on a photo are proportional to its projection onto the difference between the two source text vectors or the difference between the two target text vectors. Compute the Pearson correlation rho of these two log odds over the unlabeled photo gallery, using just its 512-dimensional covariance. Under unit-variance linear prediction this correlation is the slope between the standardized measurements. Using it to weight a *probability* contrast in a sketch is a transfer hypothesis. No parallel sketches or category names are used.

Multiply the within-pair contrast by rho while preserving the coarse term exactly. The resulting 2-by-2 block on query word probabilities has diagonal (1+rho)/2 and off-diagonal (1-rho)/2. It is nonnegative and column-stochastic for any correlation in [-1,1], including an inverted contrast. Controls include identity, averaging each pair, swapping it, the same mean rho for all pairs, a seeded shuffle of rhos across pairs, and rho from the text vectors alone. Log paired vocabulary coverage and the empirical distribution of rho.

FREEDOM already uses a discrete public vocabulary in training-free cross-domain retrieval. Cross-lingual embedding work exploits shared geometry across different word spaces. The proposed change uses two existing prompt banks to test the observability of each local distinction on the gallery. A win over the original score alone would not distinguish calibration from generic smoothing.
