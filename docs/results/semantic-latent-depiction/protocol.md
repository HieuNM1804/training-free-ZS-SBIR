# Can one depicted concept identify an incomplete view that predicts another?

The absorbing-null semantic model requires the absolute word evidence to shrink after deletion. On both Sketchy pilots, every photo violated at least one necessary inequality. CLIP can give an object **more** semantic mass when a competing region disappears. Model a latent partial view of each photograph, and obtain its word distribution by re-encoding the partial image, with the pretrained encoder performing its own renormalization. No monotonicity of individual words is imposed. Content and visibility are separated by an intervention on which native visual tokens are available. This finite test uses the 16 subsets of four corner blocks with the central cross retained and the original positions preserved, already encoded in the regional study. Each subset has equal prior probability. Checkpoints, both sets of prompts, vocabulary and draws are fixed. The hypothesis is about a joint distribution of **distinct** semantic observations, not a better crop-selection score.

For a candidate d, let b_m be its distribution over all public words under view m. One word U is sampled from the complete native sketch readout. Use only its discrete identity to infer the hidden view via p(m|U,d) proportional to b_m(U). Before accessing any other drawing, predict a distinct word V via the mixture of b_m(V)/(1−b_m(U)) over the inferred view. Test V under the whole-sketch readout of another human drawing of the true source, conditional on V differing from U. Also report V under the original sketch as an internal endpoint. Average over 128 seeded U draws, evaluating the full conditional distribution of V exactly per draw. Word sampling is shared across methods and competing photos. Redraws, source groups and labels enter evaluation only. The forecasting unit is nats. The native frozen photo, a fresh independent view for V, an independent view of the same posterior size, and a wrong view of the same size with the inferred view excluded are the controls. Masks of size 0 and 4 have only one alternative and stay unchanged. The size control preserves the complete posterior over observed area, removing only specific part identity. Verify that an uninformative observation restores the prior and that identical view distributions give identical forecasts.

Consider each true source versus the same preselected hard photo rival as in the preceding frozen source identification experiments. Average observations within a source, and bootstrap source-photo pairs so both query directions stay together. Separately quantify the posterior KL from the uniform prior, its decomposition into size and within-size identification, and the proportion of posterior probability in singleton size strata. Primary scientific evidence requires positive own-source proper-score gain over the full photo, independent views and size-only transfer on independent redraws in both splits, plus larger true-minus-foil separation than size-only transfer. Inspect within- and across-class tasks individually. An improvement in every candidate's forecast equally is inadequate for retrieval. A human omission mask is not observed; a positive result would establish predictive dependence under CLIP, not prove how a person decided what to draw.

Prior art includes generative reasoning about occlusion and partial evidence in CompositionalNets, and resolving excess reference detail in training-free CIR in STiTch. The test here enumerates frozen counterfactual views of one candidate and checks the predictive consequence of sharing the same unobserved view across two distinct concepts.
