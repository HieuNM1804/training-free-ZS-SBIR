# Can a sketch answer an image distinction that survives a domain intervention?

In the existing Cross-Domain Semantic Retrieval branch, estimate which *binary decision about two actual photographs* is identifiable from one sketch. The input is a complete sketch and two whole candidate photos. The previous patch substitution test predicted an unobserved sketch strip worse after adding the correct photo. This asks a different question. A candidate defines what needs distinguishing. It supplies no sketch token and predicts no human drawing.

For candidates A and B, let aₛ, aₜ, bₛ, bₜ be categorical CLIP distributions over the identical public vocabulary. The subscript changes only the two native text templates per domain. Both use the actual candidate pixels. Read the entire sketch with the source templates to give q. Define a randomized semantic question by its signed answer g(w) ∈ [−1,1]. After seeing word w it answers A with probability (1+g(w))/2. The same function of the word is used under both domain descriptions. With bars denoting the equal mixture of the two prompt readings, require

    (aₛ − aₜ)·g = (bₛ − bₜ)·g = 0,      (ā + b̄)·g = 0.

The first equalities remove a prompt effect separately under each hypothesized identity. The last makes the two photo errors equal in the mean reading. Maximize m = (ā−b̄)·g/2 over this set. Then (1+m)/2 is the optimum one-word probability of answering correctly for *each photo and either tested description*. The origin is feasible. This is an exact finite decision problem at inference time. No labels, sketch calibration, gradients or fitted model weights enter it. The two native prompt descriptions are proxies. Invariance under rewording a photo does not entail invariance under human drawing.

## What evidence actually forces the sketch answer?

Let M be the optimum without a sketch constraint and M₀ the optimum after adding q·g = 0. For a maximizing answer g*, the signed certificate is sign(q·g*) (M−M₀)/2 in accuracy units. A positive certificate states how much photo discrimination one must sacrifice before a question can make this sketch indifferent between A and B. If there were equally good opposite answers, their convex combination would tie and M₀=M. Thus a nonzero certificate has an orientation independent of the choice among optimal questions. A tie, or an optimum gap within primal/dual numerical uncertainty, is reported as a tie. Changing the labels A and B changes only the sign. If R contains the equality rows and c=(ā−b̄)/2, the LP dual gives M=minᵧ ‖c−Rᵀy‖₁. Report primal feasibility and dual gaps for both optima. Conditional rates extend by linearity to any mixtures of the two modeled prompt readings. Cases outside that family are empirical tests.

## Controls and falsification

Two limiting cases can be solved without benchmark data. With just two words, take A's probabilities of emitting the first word as 0.55 and 0.85 across the prompts, and B's as 0.15 and 0.45. The pooled balanced test answers according to word identity and has 70% accuracy, but within each photo the invariant question must assign both words the same answer. The equal-error constraint then forces 50% accuracy. More generally M vanishes exactly when c lies in the linear span of R's rows. Conversely, use four words for two semantic identities crossed with two styles. Let A assign 80% to the first identity, B assign 20%, and both prompts redistribute probability between its two styles in the same 9:1 and 1:9 ratios for either identity. Counting either style as the same identity gives 80% accuracy under every prompt. When q equals the source reading of A, forcing it to tie leaves just 50% photo accuracy, so the certificate is +30 percentage points. If q puts all mass on a fifth word unseen in either photo, the question can answer the sketch either way at no cost and the certificate is zero. These are algebraic examples; they carry no evidence about the real benchmarks.

1. Solve the identical problem using the same mean prompt distributions with only the equal-error constraint. This is the primary control. Compare accuracy and evidence strength on the same queries. Average dot-product word agreement with the photo-prompt distribution is an additional direct retrieval reference.
2. Read both photos with bare word prompts withheld from the solver. Record the mean absolute change of each signed photo answer from its fitted mean reading. A small prompt gap that fails to generalize to bare wording would weaken even the linguistic explanation.
3. Use naturally paired source photos and two independent human sketches per source from the existing fixed trials. Measure how well predicted answers transfer to actual drawings, their absolute calibration error against the source-photo answer, and agreement of the two human drawings. Separate within-category source identification from across-category identification. Each scoring call receives only its one sketch. Drawings never help choose the question.
4. The more important retrieval endpoint is the distinct-source category boundary cohort. Sample up to five eligible queries per class with seed 42 from the fixed two-direction cohort for this first pass, retaining both directions. Likewise sample up to 24 unordered source pairs per task mode with seed 42, retaining both query directions and redraws. The primary category comparison is invariant minus pooled-control balanced accuracy, with paired bootstrap over classes. Report any ties, newly correct and newly wrong decisions, and within-class source results separately. The two Sketchy splits overlap. The gallery-pair protocol is exploratory and does not produce benchmark mAP.
5. The scientific claim of crossing the depiction gap needs better human-drawing calibration and better category discrimination on both splits, with uncertainty reported. If prompt invariance reproduces under a third prompt but these endpoints fail, it identifies a property of prompting rather than a solution to depiction loss. No TU-Berlin selection or test is made at this stage.

The conditional-rate constraints are related to Hardt, Price and Srebro. Their setting allows decisions conditioned on group membership. Here a single word-to-answer map is imposed for two readings of unlabeled photos; the cost of forcing a sketch to tie derives directly from its feasible set. Training-free CIR systems can use a supplied modification text and reason about candidates; SBIR supplies no such text. WISER provides neighboring prior art on multimodal candidate verification. These differences do not establish priority over all related work. For the exact calculation, temporarily give groups of words the same answer. This is a restriction on the primal and supplies a lower bound. For the grouped solution's multipliers, evaluate the L1 dual bound over *all* words. In a group the gap between the sum of absolute reduced costs and their absolute sum is positive only if positive and negative terms coexist. Split each such group by sign and solve again. Stop when the two bounds agree to the numerical tolerance or fall back to the full linear program. This procedure leaves the decision problem unchanged. Validate signed certificates against the full solver for both controls, on one preselected real case for every cohort and split.
