# Which observation accounts for regional decisions at the category boundary?

The original regional module was more discriminative than held-out local word forecasts on naturally contested category ranks. The gallery dictionary and index can themselves carry strong category semantics. Isolate the information supplied by acquiring more observations of a single sketch, then isolate the candidate's use of variation across those observations. The experiment diagnoses the regional component and keeps the three-component baseline fixed.

Use the same 768 queries per split and native semantic top-200 pairing from the category-boundary study. Keep the 64 public heads and target bank identical for all arms. F is the score obtained when all windows are replaced by the two global query views, preserving the two poses. U is the score from all true windows with fixed region priors. R is the exact candidate-conditioned native region score. Reconstruct all three through the same CandidateRegions class and require R to replay the prior run. Per query the score difference R−F decomposes exactly into U−F (a change of observations) and R−U (a candidate-dependent conditional increment). Directions for the incremental terms are decisions on pair margins, not independent calibrated relevance scores. Directly compare F, U, R and both increments on all natural disjoint adjacent mixed-label pairs, with separate strata for original semantic successes and failures; primary metric is the balanced within-query accuracy for queries with both types. Report paired class intervals, original KD metrics for F/U/R, and sibling-image effects only as a separate future question.

Use a further controlled intervention: construct weights for each window using the *candidate's* local cross-word likelihood, but replace the word content of every candidate for this selection only by the same candidate-independent gallery mean bank. That construction makes the gallery-normalized attention ratio identically one, recovering U. As an additional nondegenerate control, reassign the specific choosing candidate to another photo of adjacent rank under F, preserving the very same observed regions and scoring photo. A stable common sort by F and a seed-42 alternating adjacent pairing, fixed for the query, provide the donor; any unmatched last candidate maps to itself. This preserves the local semantic level of the selecting candidate. A positive R–U gain without a gain against this matched wrong identity cannot establish that *which* candidate made the choice mattered. No labels enter these scores.

Test whether R's full-gallery mAP and conditional category discrimination exceed both U and the adjacent-candidate control in both splits. Test separately whether the R−U margin has information at the native-class boundary after taking F and U into account. This uses previously observed category-boundary trials and is exploratory. SCAN and earlier candidate-conditioned retrieval show the general principle of local alignment; this intervention is a condition-specific attribution and does not claim a new attention mechanism.
