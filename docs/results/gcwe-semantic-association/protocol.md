# Which part of the geometric posterior actually changes the retrieval ranking?

On Sketchy Ext-1 confirmation, count-16 raised direct mAP by 0.97 points. After reassigning patch histograms to unrelated gallery photos, the increase was still 0.87 points. This motivates a specific ambiguity in the inference model. Its posterior may benefit from class-independent information about whether a name is well represented in the gallery. Test it on the two pre-existing development pilots without choosing another exponent or tuning on transfer data.

Let B_dw be photo d's probability of word w, t_w=Σd B_dw its total support, H_d the photo's geometric event distribution and U the known grid prior. The measured law is L_w=(Σd B_dw H_d+U)/(t_w+1). Under uniformly random reassignment of the H_d to the same reference photos, the expected law is exactly N_w=(t_w Hbar+U)/(t_w+1). Although the reassignment destroys association, the expected law still varies with w whenever U differs from the gallery mean Hbar. Consequently even a null *without word-specific physical geometry* can change the inferred word probabilities. The expectation here is over the law before applying a log or posterior normalization.

For the locked count-16 interpretation, its log evidence separates exactly into Σ Hq log N_w and Σ Hq log(L_w/N_w), both multiplied by 16. This equality holds for the odds of every word pair. Rank all photos with each term alone, both terms together, and neither. Repeat the null term using Hbar instead of Hq to see whether *individual sketch geometry* is needed for it to work. Recompute the original four-arm interaction in mAP on matched queries; nonlinear ranking effects need not sum. The association term alone compares the measured and counterfactual observation laws as a Bayes factor. It does not claim to be a normalized distribution of geometry over events. All controls preserve gallery folds, CLIP features, word priors, native transforms and the original KD evaluator. These are exploratory semantic mechanism controls on the original 1,536 pilot queries, not a new official score or a new fourth method.
