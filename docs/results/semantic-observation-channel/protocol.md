# What semantic information survives arbitrary word-specific omission?

A richer photo may contain words that the sketch systematically omits. Model the probability of reporting word w in region r as q_r(w) ∝ η(w)b_r(w), where b is the candidate photo and η is an unknown, pair-specific reporting probability shared across regions. Any strictly positive vector is equivalent to a survival vector bounded by one after a common rescaling. The assumption of shared accessibility across regions is strong and testable. This is a model of the two CLIP readouts. The latent probabilities are not human word-annotation rates.

The odds ratio for words u,v and regions r,s is invariant under this entire channel. Conditional on the unordered words, the probability of the assignment u at r, v at s is σ(log b_r(u)−log b_s(u)−log b_r(v)+log b_s(v)). Ties contribute the same constant to every candidate. Under the model this equals the corresponding source probability. This creates a conditional predictive task with an exact positive control, and a score immune to candidate-specific, word-dependent visibility. The observation model predicts a second outcome: three observed source regions determine the missing fourth word distribution from a photo by multiplying b_t by the geometric mean of q_r/b_r on the three seen regions and normalizing. This plug-in forecast is exact if the shared-channel assumption holds. Test it against the actual sketch crop, the unmodified photo forecast, context-only prediction, and using the other photo to infer the multiplier. A good held-out forecast and actual discrimination are distinct endpoints.

For unknown alignment, place a uniform prior over the eight symmetries of a square. Observe a semantic assignment from two source corners and forecast the assignment of a different pair of words between the two *unseen* corners. Treat unordered word identities as design variables. For every context-word pair the orientation-averaged probability of its assignment is exactly 1/2 by exchange symmetry. Thus the posterior orientation is proportional to that orientation's assignment probability; average the posterior over the source crop probabilities. Forecast held-out assignments under this posterior. Use all six ways to choose the observed unordered region pair. Controls discard the orientation observation (exactly 1/2), reverse the observed assignment, use all 24 bijections, and supply fixed coordinates. Average over all word draws exactly, preserving the original soft word probabilities. This uses only one sketch per candidate. Verify the symmetry identities, normalization, exact forecasting under artificial reporting bias and numerical invariance of decisions to independently deleting candidate words. Reindexing already encoded descriptors tests alignment, not rotation of CLIP pixels.

Evaluate source identification on up to six new disjoint same-class photo pairs per class with their real redraws, plus a cross-category control using the same pool. Exclude every experimental source photo used in the two earlier regional/source pairing cohorts in either Sketchy split. Pair by highest visual cosine using the same deterministic disjoint construction, then sample with seed 42. Predeclare two mechanistic gates. For the deletion model require positive true-source held-out log gain against the unmodified photo and higher true-minus-foil advantage than the swapped multiplier on both splits. For the orientation inference require true assignment log gain above chance and source discrimination above that of arbitrary bijections on both same-class splits. Report per-sketch identification, within-pair assignment and paired intervals. If content-dependent omission fails, the algebraic invariance is an exact property of the hypothetical channel only. TU will not be used to choose the observation model.

Selection-invariant odds are classical, and prior-shift correction in ML has precedent. The candidate-specific missing-word model, its constrained counterfactual forecast and the conditional semantic assignment it implies are the claims being tested here.
