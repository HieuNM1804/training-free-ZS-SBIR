# Can GCWE share the evidence of related words while preserving the original query odds?

The preceding matched-corruption experiment found that moving the same query mass between nearby names is much less damaging than moving it between frequency-matched names. Smoothing the global CLIP word vector itself still reduced retrieval. A different hypothesis is that the feedback ratio estimated from the small donor pool is noisier than the original query distribution. Nearby terms may share a rate of semantic enrichment after spatial verification even when their original probabilities should remain distinct.

For a pair of reciprocal text-space neighbors, let O_i,O_j denote the geometric word counts and U_i,U_j their reference counts (including the frozen smoothing prior). The shared-rate estimate is (O_i+O_j)/(U_i+U_j). This maximizes the two Poisson exposure log-likelihood terms under a common rate for fractional pseudocounts. The Poisson objective is a working model: the CLIP word probabilities sum to one and are statistically dependent. Apply the shared rate separately to the existing sketch probabilities p_i and p_j. Their posterior odds ratio within the pair remains exactly p_i/p_j. Unpaired words retain their original GCWE update. The same construction using adjacent gallery-frequency pairs on exactly the same word support controls for variance reduction from pooling. Setting all feedback ratios on the paired support to one controls for simply disabling noisy evidence.

Run on seed-42 positions 2,304 through 3,839 of each independently permuted Sketchy split. The queries are disjoint within each split from both earlier diagnostic samples in this round. Use the complete gallery and original KD evaluator. The primary score includes the frozen semantic and regional terms. Advance to full evaluation if the semantic pairing improves equal-split mean mAP over original GCWE and beats both frequency pooling and disabling the affected updates on each split. A failure against frequency pooling weakens the case that semantic neighborhoods explain the change.

The update modifies the existing spatially verified relevance feedback mechanism. FREEDOM supplies the cross-domain discrete-word retrieval precedent and Chum et al. the spatial-verification feedback precedent.
