# Geometry-conditioned evidence for public words

The motivating outcome is an error of query interpretation in the two exploratory Sketchy splits. Geometry can identify certain correct gallery intermediaries which the strong score misses, but choosing just one and adding a fixed correction has little attainable mAP gain. This experiment changes how spatial evidence contributes to the meaning of the query. It uses all 200 visual nominees, regardless of the source-vs-photo prompt response, removing the upper-half partition used in the previous diagnostic.

For a single sketch, form the original two-view CLIP source word distribution. Nominate the top 200 gallery images by CLIP visual cosine. For each target photo, use donors from the opposite half of a fixed, unlabeled gallery partition. Compare their 49 frozen CLIP patches to the original sketch. The geometric measurement is distance correlation under two independent draws from a distribution on patch matches, as in the preceding branch. The name “distance correlation” here denotes an ordinary Pearson correlation of two random pairwise distances.

The reference distribution samples one donor uniformly with replacement within the eligible nominations. The alternative samples m donors from that same proposal and takes the one with highest geometry score. For tied ranks with cumulative masses a and b, the selection likelihood ratio is (b^m−a^m)/(b−a). This gives a unit-mean ratio under the reference for every query. For each word, marginalize that ratio using the donors' frozen word posteriors. An additional image's worth of opposite-fold word prior supplies smoothing. Reweight the original query word probabilities with these word-level ratios and project them onto target-photo word probabilities. Recompute the existing RGB propagation and replace its log-evidence contribution in the regional base at its inherited coefficient of 0.65. Propagation can subsequently pass evidence between folds. No other query or benchmark class name participates in estimation.

m=2 is the direct pairwise hypothesis, m=8 tests more concentrated selection, and m=32 is exploratory for true and blank-subtracted geometry. Controls with the same selection distribution use whole-image cosine, direct words, existing strong scores, a uniform-canvas sketch or permuted spatial coordinates. Alternatives omit the nomination-frequency correction or replace the sketch word prior with the gallery prior. Large changes which also occur after spatial permutation cannot be attributed to the geometric ordering. Fold-to-fold correlation of the query-supported word likelihoods measures whether independent donor subsets agree. Compare original baselines, controls and per-class differences in addition to mAP.

The elementary selection law is derived directly from the discrete maximum order statistic. The motivating literature uses spatial agreement for matching and word distributions estimated from the retrieved set for relevance feedback. The conditional interpretation of evidence under two donor-selection mechanisms is the contribution under test. [Cho et al., CVPR 2015](https://www.cv-foundation.org/openaccess/content_cvpr_2015/html/Cho_Unsupervised_Object_Discovery_2015_CVPR_paper.html). Both Sketchy splits are exploratory, with 768 queries per split and the full galleries in this pilot. The primary development comparison weighs mAP equally across splits.
