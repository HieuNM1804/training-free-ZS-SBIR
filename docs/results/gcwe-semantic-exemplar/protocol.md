# Does one scene interpretation require a single, coherent geometric exemplar?

The preceding GCWE model summarizes each scene word by a single mean distribution of spatial events. For a word with genuinely different shapes, that mean can combine mutually exclusive interpretations. Test a different generative statement within GCWE. A word predicts a distribution over real, unlabeled reference-photo geometries. The sketch can supply several observations about one of these geometries. Under the matched alternative, the same word can redraw its exemplar independently for each observation. The two laws agree for a single observation. This isolates a consequence of geometric variation *within* a word.

In each native CLIP patch grid, sample an anchor and an unordered pair of two other positions with different radii from the anchor, uniformly over eligible triples. The question b is the pair of squared radii. The answer y is whether the closer position has higher CLIP affinity to the anchor than the farther position. Enumerate all eligible triples, giving ties half credit. Let p_d(b) be the probability of a positive answer in photo d under b, with a symmetric Beta(1/2,1/2) prior. Use the same readout p_q for one sketch. The distribution h(b) of available questions is fixed by the grid. This construction conditions on location availability and discards raw similarity scale. The input is the native RGB or sketch CLIP grid; there is no boundary extraction.

Let p0(b) be the mean answer in the opposite reference-gallery fold. Express the query's uncertain answer as virtual evidence, using likelihoods p_q/p0 for positive and (1−p_q)/(1−p0) for negative outcomes. Their common scale is immaterial to inference. The integrated likelihood for one observation from d is exactly

    K(q,d) = 1 + Σb h(b) (p_q(b)−p0(b)) (p_d(b)−p0(b)) / [p0(b)(1−p0(b))].

K equals one for every reference photo if the sketch supplies only background-level answers. It is nonnegative by its two-outcome construction and its reference-gallery mean is exactly one. For each public concept w use the fixed distribution P(d|w) proportional to the CLIP word probability of w in each reference photo. One shared exemplar for m virtual observations gives likelihood E[K^m|w]. Independent exemplars give E[K|w]^m. Their difference comes entirely from within-word variation of K. Multiply the prior whole-sketch word distribution by the inferred likelihood, normalize, and score every candidate from the other gallery fold by agreement with its native word distribution. Both arms infer semantic probabilities; neither uses a geometric term added directly to a rank score.

Use m=16 as the primary effective repeat count inherited from the previous study and m=1,4,64 as prespecified sensitivities. Compare also to permuting semantic distributions among the same reference photos, preserving all concept masses and geometric evidence, and to the original word channel. Verify permutation symmetries, orientation ties, exact binary-likelihood equivalence, null-query identity, and the one-observation equality. Report reference-geometry dispersion and concentration of the exemplar posterior. The m repeated observations are an evidence model; the 49 contextual tokens are not 49 independent physical measurements.

Evaluate 1,536 seed-42 Sketchy queries per split, against the entire official gallery, with the unchanged KD evaluator. Promote a variant only if it improves both mAPs over the word baseline and the independent-exemplar model. If the independent-exemplar law matches or wins, the original hypothesis about variation within a word fails this retrieval test. The geometry association must also outperform the shuffled assignment. If multiple variants qualify, choose by equal-split mean mAP and then precision. Lock the choice before remaining queries and held-out TU-Berlin. Labels never enter scoring. Bayesian Sets motivates comparing a common hidden explanation to independent explanations. Local self-similarity descriptors are established cross-depiction signals. The proposed contribution lies in the word-conditioned latent exemplar, conditional ordinal evidence and its experimentally distinguishable implications.
