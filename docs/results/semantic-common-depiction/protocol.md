# Can two disjoint observations of one sketch be explained by a common hidden depiction of a candidate?

The preceding forecasting assay treated a sampled sketch word u as known to belong to every candidate, including photos that assigned u a tiny probability. A wrong photo could then select an unlikely view and improve its forecast more than the right one. In actual retrieval there is an uncertain sketch observation and a candidate hypothesis. Test the alternative generative procedure on a single query. Conditional on candidate d, draw a partial depiction m, draw one public word from its candidate readout h_m, and independently draw one from query observation A. The observation E_A is **agreement of these draws**, without disclosing the word identity. Thus P(m|E_A,d) is proportional to ρ_m times the lexical overlap of A with h_m. An improbable match contributes little to that view's posterior. Compare two hypotheses: the independent word matches with query observations A,B must come from the same partial depiction versus two newly drawn depictions. A and B comprise disjoint sets of sketch patch tokens and disjoint sets of public word identities. No second query, redraw, gallery label, or training update is used.

Write Z_mA = q_A·h_m. The shared likelihood is Σ_m ρ_m Z_mA Z_mB. The independent likelihood is (q_A·a)(q_B·a) exactly because the word marginal a is fixed to the native intact photo. Their Bayes factor equals Σ_m P(m|E_A)P(m|E_B)/ρ_m. This specifies the observable events, what represents missing photo content, and where the candidate's relevance prior enters. Forcing either answer to be a known word induces a different posterior. The two hypotheses cannot be distinguished with a single agreement event. A size-only control shares just the number of parts (independent views within size). A false correspondence shuffles latent view identities independently for each word within size; it exactly preserves the candidate's intact semantic marginal. Report all controls as complete paired likelihoods. Also report the statistic obtained by forcing the sketch word and then predicting the second agreement, for comparison under the same first-event evidence.

Use left versus right (and top versus bottom) disjoint 3×7 strips in the native 7×7 grid. Exclude the middle row or column separating each pair. The text-only split uses the previously fixed seed-42 semantic axis; the first strip can report words in one half and the other strip words in the other half. Swap the vocabulary halves, giving four equally likely assignments over two spatial partitions. Normalize query words separately on each allowed vocabulary. Marginalize the likelihood over the four assignments. Every legitimate joint model is symmetric in which observation is received first. Validate both expansions of the Bayes factor, reduction to the intact base for independent views, neutral observations, and commutativity under swapping A,B.

Test on the natural category boundary cohort with source-disjoint photos already selected for another mechanism, dropping original reference-photo candidates. This is exploratory reuse of an assessment cohort, not a new blind test. Scores for both candidates are computed before accessing class labels. Report resolution of previously wrong vs previously correct decisions, balanced accuracy on queries that have both, and per-class intervals for differences. Primary requirement: improvement in balanced accuracy against the exact independent, size-only and false-correspondence controls on both splits, and a favorable correct-minus-foil log evidence change over independent. Report how often a likelihood ratio actually changes a decision. Inspect cases where a common depiction can explain both pieces of the sketch for the wrong candidate. The pairwise assessment does not measure full-gallery mAP of the new model. A deployable score exists per single query/photo pair, so a positive component test can proceed to full-gallery retrieval.
