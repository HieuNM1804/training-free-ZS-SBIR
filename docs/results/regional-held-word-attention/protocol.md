# Can other concepts localize the word being scored?

The frozen candidate-conditioned reader assigns an affinity to region r of query q and candidate i, L(r,i) = sum over words w of c(r,i,w). Each contribution c is the product of the region's word activation and the candidate's compact word weight. The candidate-specific attention is proportional to the fixed region prior times L(r,i) divided by its gallery mean. The original reader then scores L using that same attention. Thus a single word can select its own region and simultaneously supply the evidence read from that region.

For each of the 64 readout words, exclude that word from both L and its gallery mean while calculating attention, and use the resulting attention to read just that word. Sum the 64 readouts. This construction preserves the original 13 crops, two poses, priors, gallery bank and all coefficients. A stable sum of the contributions on either side of a held word computes the excluded total even if the held word dominates. Excluding a different, gallery-frequency-adjacent word per readout is a matched sham. Every word is held once in each condition. Reconstruct the original gate algebraically and compare its score with the frozen implementation.

Candidate-conditioned alignment has prior art in SCAN. ACoL demonstrates complementary localization by erasing spatial evidence during training. Here the held-out variable is a public concept in a frozen pair scorer. The evidence being scored is retained; its influence on the localization gate is the single intervention. This tests whether cross-concept agreement carries more transferable evidence than a dominant word selecting itself. [SCAN, ECCV 2018](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_ECCV_2018/html/Kuang-Huei_Lee_Stacked_Cross_Attention_for_Image-Text_Matching_ECCV_2018_paper.html) · [ACoL, CVPR 2018](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_cvpr_2018/html/Zhang_Adversarial_Complementary_Learning_CVPR_2018_paper.html)

Use 1,536 seed-42 queries per Sketchy split and complete galleries. Evaluate the regional term alone and the frozen semantic-plus-regional score. The primary comparison is held versus original in the regional term; a higher equal-split mean and gains against the sham in both splits justify further development. If erasing the scored word is worse than erasing a frequency-matched other word, that is evidence of useful self-localization. These arms omit GCWE to directly isolate the regional hypothesis. No TU selection. Report both metrics and uncertainty under paired class resampling.
