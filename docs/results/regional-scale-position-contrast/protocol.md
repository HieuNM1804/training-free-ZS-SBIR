# Does a candidate benefit from a position independently of its preferred scale?

Use the original 13-window reader on both poses. Factor attention into scale mass and conditional position at that scale. Either factor can come from that candidate or the query-specific gallery mean attention. Write L00, L10, L01, L11 for the four resulting log scores (scale and position indicators in that order). I = L11 - L10 - L01 + L00 is the log cross-ratio. It measures nonadditivity of the readout in log evidence; it is not a statistical independence or causal test.

The scale-only standalone result replicated on held-apart Sketchy queries and TU, but the full gate performed better with global semantics. Hypothesis: candidate position can be actively misleading averaged across scales, while its dependence on a candidate's chosen scale conveys additional information. Test L10 + I, which retains candidate-specific scale and interaction and removes the position main effect L01-L00. Compare with L11, L10, the purely additive score L10+L01-L00, and L10 plus a different photo’s I under a seed-42 fixed-point-free permutation. The last control preserves the per-query distribution of the interaction. No coefficients are fitted. Recompose the baseline as an algebra check.

Primary outcome: with the original global semantic weight, L10+I must exceed L11 in equal-split mean mAP, and exceed both L10 and its permuted-interaction control on each split. If it qualifies, confirm on disjoint queries before testing the complete GCWE system and locked TU transfer. Report regional-only results separately. Pilot: first 1,536 queries of each seed-42 permutation; full galleries; original KD metrics. Earlier studies used these queries, so this is exploratory. The module retains candidate-conditioned matching, a mechanism with prior art in [SCAN, ECCV 2018](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_ECCV_2018/html/Kuang-Huei_Lee_Stacked_Cross_Attention_for_Image-Text_Matching_ECCV_2018_paper.html).
