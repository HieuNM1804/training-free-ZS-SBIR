# Is a candidate's semantic response useful when the uncertainty about coordinates is held fixed?

The post-hoc source analysis found positive information in geometric matching confidence, but transported lexical forecasts lost the source advantage. Each competing photo chose its own geometric posterior. A sharper posterior can penalize the true photo more heavily when its lexical responses are only weakly calibrated. Exchange the two geometric posteriors while holding photo word responses fixed. Compute all four geometric-by-semantic combinations on exactly the same drawings, operations and word draws. Also compare both photos under a **single symmetric mixture** of their pre-intervention posteriors, applied before computing proper loss. This poses a different inference hypothesis: the pair is tested under a common uncertain coordinate convention. The two candidate photos enter symmetrically and no other query is used. The experiment defines a pair decision, and does not by itself define a scalable transitive full-gallery ordering.

Report the average true-minus-foil Brier advantage when coordinates vary with the scored photo, when the posteriors are exchanged, and when the common mixture is used. Calculate separately the two fixed-geometry semantic contrasts, averaging them for the semantic main effect. Hold each semantic readout fixed while swapping geometry for the geometric main effect. Preserve the sign convention “positive favors the true photo”. The sum of the two main effects equals the native Brier advantage exactly. Retain the difference-of-differences to assess whether the effect of changing geometry depends on whose word scores it interprets. The main endpoint is a positive semantic main effect with pair-bootstrap intervals excluding zero on both splits; also require source discrimination under a common mixture to improve on the initial candidate-specific order on both splits before attributing the loss to candidate-dependent calibration. Keep natural and mirrored queries separate.

For each tested action pair, choose an involution h of the square that exchanges the two actions. For the fixed-map semantic assignment probabilities r(gh)=1−r(g). Therefore the margin of the Bayesian forecast around 1/2 has absolute value at most TV(π,π∘h)/2, for every observed pair of words. Verify the identity and bound numerically. Report the geometry-only limit, the realized average absolute margin and the signed margin for correct-source assignments. On draws from the true assignment, writing z=p−1/2 gives the exact Brier gain over chance E[z]−E[z²]. This separates whether the word directions agree from overconfidence in their amplitude. No coefficient or temperature is estimated. This analysis follows the original observation and is exploratory. [Original action transfer](../gcwe-transported-intervention/protocol.md)
