# Which paths let two observed parts identify a photograph?

Freeze the inputs and evaluator of the full-gallery [equal-patch intervention](../semantic-context-union/README.md). It compared extrapolation from two separate encodings with a joint encoding of the very same patches. It left open whether the gain requires the patches in one part to receive information about the other part. Test three communication graphs in the original twelve frozen OpenAI CLIP ViT-B/32 blocks. Preserve each patch's absolute position, layer normalization, token embeddings, residual paths and pretrained projections. All gallery features and the 20,931-word readout remain fixed.

1. The two patch groups evolve independently, each with its own copy of the native CLS. A third, shared read-only CLS attends to its own history and all 42 patch states in every layer. Patches have no path back from this readout. Every local patch receives exactly the information it would receive when encoding its own strip separately.
2. Replace the private summaries by the single shared CLS. It receives information from both sides and now feeds that information back to each patch. Patches still cannot attend directly across the gap.
3. Restore every native attention edge, including direct cross-part patch exchange. Verify against the joint encoding from the previous cohort. Also compare against the previous additive baseline, with equal IDs, gallery, axes, and metric implementation.

The ordered mAP differences isolate what becomes possible when adding each pathway in this intervention. Attention renormalizes over the allowed keys when an edge is changed. These differences therefore concern retrievability under the three graphs and depend on their order. They do not measure additive fractions of all CLIP computation or prove the states carry a particular human concept. Use identical prompts, score and aggregation for both axes in all arms. Log both official metrics and a paired, class-equal bootstrap on AP. A large positive direct-edge contrast on both splits would support the claim that context-sensitive regional states matter beyond a native recurrent global reader. If the blocked-direct-edge arm matches joint reading, prioritize the shared reader or its feedback. A read-only arm close to joint reading would instead make independent region indexing plausible. No new retrieval score or coefficients are selected.

CaiT distinguishes learning interactions between patches from reading patches into a class embedding. Here we intervene in pretrained CLIP without training a readout. BASIC illustrates late fusion of two genuine retrieval observations. This test asks how much is lost if a single observation is split before encoding.
