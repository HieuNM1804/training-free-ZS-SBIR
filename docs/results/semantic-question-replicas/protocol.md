# Can the same semantic decision survive a different photograph of a hypothesized object?

The pilot's binary word questions separated the *two named photo instances* at 93–95% accuracy per categorical word draw, yet their verdict on the class boundary was only 36–48%. The alternatives in a category retrieval task include other instances. Instead of enforcing invariance between two text descriptions of one image, test a robust binary decision over alternative **real photographs** selected without class labels. This stays within Cross-Domain Semantic Retrieval. It is a pairwise inference experiment without a learned classifier, modified backbone, rank fusion or query-dependent selection of gallery neighbors.

Partition the official gallery reproducibly into two panels with seed 42. For each rival, take its nearest other frozen RGB CLIP neighbor from the opposite panel, excluding both contenders. Each side now has its original image and one possible replacement. Use the standard photo-prompt word distributions throughout the gallery and source-prompt words for the sketch. A decision assigns a signed randomized answer to each public word. Choose it to maximize the *worst* correct signed response among the four photographs. This is a minimax test over any mixtures of these two possible observations on each side. If both sides contain a common distribution the best worst-case one-word decision is exactly chance. If means separate but the two mixture families intersect, deciding the means confidently can still be misleading. The dual optimizes the L1 norm of a signed mixture of the rows, with nonnegative weights summing to one. An optimal mixture identifies which photograph limits discrimination.

As before, quantify the sacrifice in worst-case photo accuracy needed to force a sketch's answer to zero. Controls are (1) a minimax question on the two original photos only, (2) the same test on the two within-side **mean distributions**, and (3) a minimax test after exchanging the two replacement photos between the rival hypotheses. All methods get exactly the same sketch. Native and pooled controls need not have the same optima as the previous equal-error equality formulation. Test reversal of rival IDs, direct LP agreement, and a shared-distribution counterexample. Serialize both accuracy-optimization bounds, numerical residuals and least-favorable weights.

Reuse the fixed development subset of class-boundary queries with both original decision directions (96 / 94 queries). The source photograph used to draw the sketch is available **only after neighbor selection** for auditing collision and for observing actual within-class transfer. The primary assessment excludes an entire query if either nominated replacement for either of its two pairs equals that source, preserving both decision directions and avoiding inadvertent exposure. Report the all-query sensitivity check and counts. Gallery classes enter only the assessment of neighbor purity and final correctness. Report candidate performance, transfer to the matched real source photo, transfer to its sketch, coverage of non-ties, and class-equal paired intervals. A retrieval interpretation needs a positive effect over mean pooling, originals alone and exchanged replicas on both held-out-within-study sketch sets. These sets have been used for diagnostic development and are not independent confirmations. If the worst-case guarantee improves on support but fails on the source, the unlabeled hypotheses do not contain all the variation that matters. If it transfers to the source but fails on the sketch, the cross-depiction gap persists.

Goldenshluger, Juditsky and Nemirovski give a broader convex-composite hypothesis testing framework; their detector and risk need not equal this finite single-word minimax problem. WISER supplies a training-free CIR comparison that verifies candidates given explicit multimodal edits. The distinctive research question here is whether competing *sets of observed gallery instances* define a reproducible semantic decision and a verifiable loss of discriminability when a drawing gives the opposite answer. Novelty of convex hypothesis testing itself is not claimed.
