# How does an explicit “word not observed” outcome change a semantic impossibility test?

All named spatial decisions in the previous test assigned four locations to every word regardless of its semantic support. On over 99% of candidates at least one pair contradicted complete spatial degradation. Test a different observation model inside Cross-Domain Semantic Retrieval. Select one of the four regions uniformly and draw a public word from its frozen CLIP distribution over all 20,931 words (average two renderings within each region). When probing word u, emit the region if the draw equals u, else emit a null. The row of non-null probabilities Yᵤ(r) is p(u | r)/4. These are probabilities of a specified CLIP observation event, not calibrated human depiction probabilities. Use the same 64 gallery words as the prior study for both depictions.

Test the hypothesis that the sketch is produced by deletion and displacement of word evidence. A single nonnegative substochastic matrix K acts on the four regional events for every word; excess row mass is sent to null, and the null never creates a new word detection. The hypothesis gives X = YK for the non-null tables. Unlike the prior four-state experiment, words may now be absent. A one-state reduction observes only whether the word was detected. It keeps the same detection probability separately for every word and discards regional identity. The underlying family applies to any paired domains with comparable observed events and source-to-target loss. Domain calibration and the ability of drawing to emphasize a detail are genuine ways it can fail.

For one word the total detection probability aᵤ = Σᵣ Xᵤ(r) cannot increase under the hypothesized map. For two words u,v the maximal difference in probability of a positive regional event is Hᵤᵥ(X) = Σᵣ [Xᵤ(r) − Xᵥ(r)]₊. Positive-part contraction implies Hᵤᵥ(X) ≤ Hᵤᵥ(Y), even when locations move. If each simulated row is within ε of the true completed row in total variation, the unary constraint can be exceeded by at most ε and any directed binary constraint by at most 2ε. Score the largest positive lower bound, checking every single word and both directions of all 2,016 word pairs. Include unary-only, pooled binary-plus-unary, and region-specific controls. The control applies a separate fixed random permutation of four named regions for each gallery word. It preserves every per-word detection probability and concentration, changing only how **different** words relate in that image. Uniformly permuting all four regions together must leave scores invariant. The one-state case is its own valid comparison; the gap between two model bounds is not an estimator of a causal local effect. Validate both inequalities on exactly generated erasure maps, nontrivial examples, and a counterexample where transferring mass from null to a visible word breaks the absorbing-null premise.

Evaluate all arms on the same 768 independent sketches per split, complete galleries and original KD metrics. The native semantic score supplies only exact tie-breaking (and secondary order for a logical pass/fail filter at the previous numerical tolerance of 2e−6). Measure full-gallery scores, pass rates and discrimination at the native semantic category boundary. Require the located model to beat both the pooled and independent-word-permutation controls in mAP on both splits before claiming incremental regional evidence. Report query-wise equality of the unary and combined bounds and the proportion of query/photo pairs where a two-word decision actually strengthens a unary certificate. Determine whether an apparent gain is explained entirely by restoring information about word detection. No coefficient or word-activity cutoff is tuned.
