# Does spatial verification support the noun receiving credit?

Original GCWE gives all the words in one donor its same scalar verification weight. A donor can contain several concepts whose parts agree with the sketch by different amounts. Test assigning geometric credit separately to the 16 words with largest query probability. All remaining words retain their original counts. The rank budget, original and blank query patches, gallery folds, regional and global terms stay locked.

Let C_ab be the existing joint probability of sketch patch a matching photo patch b. Let mu and nu be the marginal mean distances between two independent patch draws on each side. Define H=(Dq−mu) C (Dp−nu)^T, where subtraction is elementwise by the scalar. For each photo patch b, t_b=sum_a C_ab H_ab divided by its marginal C_b and by the product of distance standard deviations. The original scalar Pearson geometry is exactly sum_b C_b t_b; this identity is checked numerically for every pair. For word w use a posterior over b proportional to C_b times exp(CLIP-scale times cosine(photo patch b, target text w)). The conditional mean of t_b under that posterior is the geometric evidence for w. Apply the same operation to the original mean-color canvas null and take the difference. Uniform word affinity recovers the original global geometry. A word-specific score ranks the opposite-fold donors for that word.

Spatial verification for query expansion and extracting local text-aligned information from frozen CLIP have precedents. Here projected native patches, rather than a modified dense backbone, supply the localization signal. The proposed claim concerns allocating an exactly decomposable structural statistic to semantic updates. It depends on defeating three matched controls: exchange word identities within the top 16, average the conditioned scores across those words, or shuffle the association of grounding logits to patch positions. The last two are distinct: generic foreground focus can help without indicating which noun was verified.
