# Does redundancy among donor interpretations bias GCWE?

The original verifier estimates which words become enriched in the maximum-geometry candidate of eight uniformly sampled nominees. Under independent draws the same common semantic interpretation can occupy most of that proposal. Test whether GCWE benefits when each of four independent *pairs* must present competing semantic interpretations, with eight total draws and the same frozen geometry statistic.

Within the opposite gallery fold let b_i be a nominee's normalized photo-word posterior and K_ij the inner product of the square roots of b_i and b_j. Sampling a size-two determinantal subset with equal qualities gives unordered pair weights 1−K_ij² and zero self-pair weight. Normalize over ordered pairs to obtain a symmetric joint law P, with marginal p. If every weight is zero use the uniform distinct-pair limit. For every geometry threshold the probability both donors lie below it is the corresponding square-prefix mass of P. The CDF of the best of four independent pairs is the fourth power of that quantity. Differencing adjacent entries gives the exact winning-donor distribution u without Monte Carlo. Equal geometry scores use the stable original nomination order in all pair arms. Validate the law against exhaustive enumeration on a small nonuniform distribution.

Replace the uniform GCWE denominator by n sum p_i b_i and the numerator by n sum u_i b_i, adding the original one-photo smoothing prior to both counts. Compare native GCWE, the same four distinct-pair draws with uniform affinities, eight independent draws with the identical endpoint marginals, and shuffled semantic pairing within each eligible fold. Resulting updates differ only in the donor proposal. The paper on determinantal processes supplies the negative dependence principle; the proposed step is an exact semantic contrast proposal for geometry-to-word feedback. [Kulesza and Taskar, NeurIPS 2010](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2010/hash/1f50893f80d6830d62765ffad7721742-Abstract.html) · [Chum et al., ICCV 2007](https://www.robots.ox.ac.uk/~vgg/publications/2007/Chum07b/)

Use the native three-module framework on 1,536 exploratory queries per Sketchy split at seed-42 permutation positions 5,376–6,911. Each split uses its full gallery and reproduces the native T4 score. Advance if mean mAP improves over GCWE and the pair law beats both marginal-only and permuted controls on each split. Also report the equal-distinct baseline, precision and class-bootstrap intervals. A difference against uniform draws alone could follow from reweighting rarer donors. No TU selection.
