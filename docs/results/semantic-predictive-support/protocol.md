# Which features of an under-specified query persist across different photographs?

The previous coupled dictionary decoded one expected target embedding. It lost substantially to the direct semantic retrieval control on both complete Sketchy galleries. Here an independent hypothesis in Cross-Domain Semantic Retrieval treats a sketch as evidence for a *property* which several target photographs may express. It does not require them to use a single word or to realize the same target appearance.

For query q and photo j, let s(j) be the agreement of their full 20,931-word CLIP distributions in their respective native prompts. Normalize this by its mean over the gallery to obtain r(j). With a uniform background on gallery identity, r is the density ratio of drawing a photo conditional on the sketch evidence to drawing an arbitrary photo. For every candidate take it and its nearest other RGB CLIP photos as a set. Under the alternative, an unknown fraction ρ of the set carries the sketch evidence. Under the null all observations follow the background. With a uniform prior over ρ, the Bayes factor is the integral over [0,1] of the product of (1−ρ+ρr(j)). This sums every possible assignment of supporting photos. A single supporting photo yields the arithmetic mean of r. Requiring all to support the query gives the product. Fixing ρ at 1/2 removes uncertainty about the fraction. The comparison holds neighbor IDs and semantic input fixed. The integrated model treats selected neighbor observations as conditionally independent. This approximation can fail on real visual neighborhoods.

Use a set of 8 photos as primary, and 4 and 16 as prespecified alternatives. Compare also an anchored version where the candidate has to support the query and the fraction applies only to neighbors. Include the repository's locked single-view RGB diffusion on identical input to measure practical competitiveness. Integrate by Gauss–Legendre quadrature, which is exact for the polynomial of bounded degree here. Check with brute-force enumeration of all support subsets before scoring real data.

Use the seeded 1,536-query pilot from the reconstruction experiment, the full KD-SBIR gallery and its evaluator. Screen by the mean of the two Sketchy mAPs. Confirm a method on the untouched remainder when it beats direct evidence and the matched one-supporter pooling rule on both pilot splits. Report the locked graph separately and disclose any deficit. Prespecify the same selection criterion for the anchored variant. Choose no parameter on TU-Berlin. No data point is flagged, filtered or corrected by the retrieval model. The unknown membership concerns semantic relevance in every candidate set.

Bayesian Sets uses a marginal likelihood ratio for shared membership. MLESAC uses explicit membership and a background component for likelihood-based inference. Here the event is a sketch's public-word observation and the marginalization is over the extent of that observation across an unlabeled target neighborhood. This has a different observable prediction from linear diffusion: neighborhoods with the same mean evidence may rank differently depending on how many other photos support the sketch.
